# Turn free role guides into practice loops

The learning and interview-prep products that keep people coming back share one design. They are not better articles. They are **short, typed units that each end in an attempt, followed by feedback, with recorded progress and a reason to return on a set day**. Duolingo, freeCodeCamp, Khan Academy, Grind 75 and LeetCode all work this way, and the learning-science literature backs the core moves: retrieval practice (g ≈ 0.5 against rereading), spacing, implementation intentions (d ≈ 0.65) and recorded progress (d = 0.40). autointerviewai.com has already built the skeleton. Its six role hubs have attempt-first answer reveals, a worked first example, "what the interviewer is listening for" boxes, a practised counter and 7/14/30-day plans with calendar export. What it lacks is the loop around that skeleton. Self-checks are a single "mark as practised" toggle. Plans block question types by day and never bring a question back. Progress counts sections scrolled past, not skill. Questions carry no source or date. And nothing on a return visit tells the learner what is due today. Fixing those gaps costs little, works in the browser with no login and no AI calls, and keeps every word server-rendered. Traffic is a separate problem. **1,000 organic clicks a day is about 1.5 times the estimated non-brand organic traffic of the category leader, Exponent** (about 19.7K visits a month), so six excellent hubs will not get there. The site needs a portfolio of question, company-by-role and comparison pages on the soft SERPs for new AI-era roles, where hiring templates and Glassdoor shells now rank and no candidate-side prep brand dominates.

## The leaders win with practice loops, not longer pages

The most-documented retention engine in learning products is Duolingo, and its biggest gains came from return mechanics, not content. Current-user retention rose 21%, daily churn among the best users fell by over 40%, and DAU grew 4.5x over four years. A growth-model simulation found that retention of current users had **5x the DAU impact of the next-best lever** ([Lenny's Newsletter, Jorge Mazal](https://lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)). The mechanics behind those numbers were small and specific. One test separated the streak from the self-chosen daily goal, so that a single lesson kept the streak alive. It lifted D14 retention 3.3% and streak-holding new learners 19%. Before the change, almost 40% of near-streak learners had picked the "intense" goal and were failing to meet it ([Duolingo Blog, 2020](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak)). Streak freezes raised daily active learners 0.38% without increasing days off ([Duolingo Blog, 2022](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit)). Notifications that rotate fresh templates beat repeated ones, but only by about 2% on new-user retention ([Yancey & Settles, KDD 2020](https://research.Duolingo.com/papers/yancey.kdd20.pdf)). Duolingo also lists its failures. A limited-moves counter took months to build and was neutral, and a referral programme added only 3% new users ([Lenny's Newsletter](https://lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)). For a role-guide site, the lesson is to make the minimum unit tiny, make big goals optional, and treat return visits as the main metric.

freeCodeCamp is the clearest public example of a curriculum built as typed units. Its 2024 full-stack path has **513 short lectures, each paired with multiple-choice questions and a transcript, plus 64 workshops, 83 test-graded labs, 62 review pages, 66 quizzes and a built-in spaced-repetition system** ([freeCodeCamp News, Oct 2024](https://www.freecodecamp.org/news/freecodecamp-turns-10-major-curriculum-updates)). Khan Academy measures progress per skill (Attempted, Familiar, Proficient, Mastered), not per page visited. Its efficacy data shows dose-response: students who used it for 30 or more minutes a week saw about 20% greater-than-expected gains, but only about 9% of students reached that level ([Khan Academy Blog, Dec 2024](https://blog.khanacademy.org/?p=18194)). Older edX data points the same way. Median video engagement topped out at about 6 minutes, and the share of learners attempting the next problem fell from 56% to 31% as videos got longer ([Guo, Kim & Rubin, L@S 2014](https://www.cs.rochester.edu/hci/pubs/pdfs/edX-MOOC-video-production-and-engagement_LAS-2014.pdf)). Put the attempt straight after a short chunk, not at the bottom of a long page.

The interview-prep products that spread organically follow the same pattern for a narrower job. Grind 75 builds a custom schedule from the hours a learner has each week, lets people view questions by topic, difficulty or week, and keeps the core list free of premium questions ([Grind 75 About](https://techinterviewhandbook.org/grind75/about)). LeetCode's Daily Challenge adds coins, monthly badges and paid "Time Travel Tickets" that repair missed days, which works as a forgiveness mechanic ([LeetCode Discuss](https://leetcode.com/discuss/general-discussion/655704/)). Each is a **named, finite, numbered list with progress and a daily reason to come back**, and nobody has built that model for non-coding roles.

The paid competitors show what to give away. Exponent, now rebranded Aced, wraps each company guide in a hub. The hub has tabs for real interview reports, a question bank and resumes, a named "Verified" author, an "updated 9 days ago" stamp and current pay data, but **no model answers on the free page** ([Exponent Google PM guide](https://www.tryexponent.com/guides/google-product-manager-interview)). Its paid tiers lock courses, written solutions, interview experiences and every AI feature. AI mock interviews sit in the $99-a-month Expert tier ([PracHub, Exponent pricing](https://prachub.com/resources/exponent-pricing)). Hello Interview ended human mocks in May 2026 ([PracHub, Hello Interview pricing](https://prachub.com/resources/hellointerview-pricing)). Aced is moving directly into autointerviewai's niches, with an AI-first PM course, a Forward Deployed Engineer course and an AI coach ([Exponent blog](https://www.tryexponent.com/blog/exponent-is-becoming-aced)). That makes it the main threat, but its best material is behind a paywall. Users' complaints mark out the gap. Content that used to be free got paywalled ([Blind on Hello Interview](https://www.teamblind.com/post/good-one-hello-interview-you-had-free-content-now-its-premium-qq8klul0)). Prep services teach "same boxed answers and structures to every problem" ([Blind on Exponent](https://www.teamblind.com/post/exponent-subscription-worth-it-for-pm-interviews-rgtyiphb)). And crowd-sourced question banks come with no guarantee of accuracy ([PracHub](https://prachub.com/resources/exponent-pricing)). A free site that shows open, reasoned answers for questions with a stated source and date answers all three complaints.

## Learning science says attempt, check, space and mix

The two most portable findings are retrieval practice and spacing. Dunlosky and colleagues rated both "high utility" and rated rereading and highlighting "low" ([Dunlosky et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266)). Practice tests beat restudy at about g = 0.51 ([Adesope et al. 2017](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654316689306)). The effect is larger with recall-type tests and with feedback ([Rowland 2014](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0037559)), and it transfers to new questions at about d = 0.40 ([Pan & Rickard 2018](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000151)). Spaced retrieval beats massed retrieval at about g = 0.74 ([Latimier et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-020-09572-8)). The best gap between sessions is roughly **10–20% of the time until the test**, so for an interview two weeks away a learner should revisit about every 2–3 days ([Cepeda et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02209.x)). These effect sizes come from the cited papers and were not all re-verified against full texts in this research. Their direction is well established.

Several moves have moderate support and depend on conditions. Interleaving averages g = 0.42 and works best when categories are easy to confuse. It hurts with word-like material, where blocking does better (g = −0.39) ([Brunmair & Richter 2019](https://www.psychologie.uni-wuerzburg.de/fileadmin/06020400/2019/Brunmair_Richter_in_press__2019_META-ANALYSIS_OF_INTERLEAVED_LEARNING.pdf)). Interview question types such as behavioural, product sense, metrics and technical design are exactly the confusable kind. Prompted self-explanation reaches g = 0.55, and **choose-from-a-list prompts were the weakest format** ([BPS summary of Bisra et al. 2018](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/self-explanation-powerful-learning-technique-according-meta-analysis-64-studies)). Pretesting, meaning a cold attempt before study followed by the answer, improved recall at d ≈ 0.46–1.20. Learners did not believe it helped unless they were told and shown ([Pan & Rivers 2023](https://sc-pan.github.io/pdf/PR_2023.pdf)). Worked examples help novices, but the benefit fades and can reverse as expertise grows ([Kalyuga et al. 2003](https://doi.org/10.1207/S15326985EP3801_4)). That supports fading support from a full example to a skeleton to the bare prompt ([Renkl & Atkinson 2003](https://doi.org/10.1207/S15326985EP3801_3)). Comparing contrasting cases adds about d = 0.50 ([Alfieri et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1080/00461520.2013.775712)). Segmenting content into coherent, learner-paced units has small-to-medium effects ([Rey et al. 2019](https://is.tuebingen.mpg.de/publications/rey2018segmenting)). The "8-second attention span" figure is a myth with no traceable research source ([WSJ/BBC compilation](https://internet.psych.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/532-Master/532-UnitPages/Unit-09/Goldfish_MythBusting.pdf)). Units should be sized by coherence, one question type at a time, not by a timer.

Motivation research points the same way. A meta-analysis of 138 RCTs found that progress-monitoring interventions improved goal attainment at **d = 0.40**, and the effect was larger when progress was physically recorded or made public ([Harkin et al. 2016](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/87431/)). If-then plans ("on Tuesday at 8 pm I will do round 2") reach about d = 0.65 ([Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006](<https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1>)). A coffee card with 2 stamps already filled was completed about 20% faster than a blank card needing the same number of purchases ([Kivetz et al. 2006](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/1200/goalgradient.pdf)). Feedback helps on average, but **over a third of feedback interventions reduced performance**, mostly when they pointed at the self rather than the task ([Kluger & DeNisi 1996](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.119.2.254)).

The interview-specific evidence is thinner but consistent. Coaching tailored to an interview's actual content raised scores. Effective strategies included thinking each question through and organising the answer before speaking ([Maurer et al. 2001](https://doi.org/10.1037/0021-9010.86.4.709)). Coached samples showed higher predictive validity, because prepared candidates expressed their real competence more fully ([HR Tests summary of Maurer et al. 2008](https://hrtests.blogspot.com/2008/03/does-interview-coaching-increase.html)). Knowing which criteria are being assessed relates to better structured-interview performance ([Schudlik et al. 2020](https://core.ac.uk/download/355872343.pdf); [Klehe et al. 2008](https://jlupub.ub.uni-giessen.de/bitstream/jlupub/17122/1/KleheTransparency_2008.pdf)). Saying material aloud improves memory over silent reading ([MacLeod et al. 2010](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0018785)). One caution matters here. Almost none of this research tests spoken, open-ended answers, so applying it to interview prep is an informed inference, not a demonstrated result.

## The hubs have the skeleton; the loop around it is missing

A read of the site's code as of 5 November 2026 shows a strong base. The six role hubs (`/aipm`, `/fde`, `/gtm-engineer`, `/ai-engineer`, `/founders-office`, `/chief-of-staff`) render from `components/roleHub/RoleHubPage.tsx` with data in `data/roleHubs/*.ts`. They carry 29–35 questions each, most with a "listening for" note. The `QuestionItem` component in `components/roleHub/JourneyClient.tsx` already keeps every answer in the server HTML and folds it behind "Your turn first: plan 20 seconds, answer out loud in 90" only after hydration. The first question stays open as a worked example. Pay tables carry a source and an "as of" date. Scroll reveals leave content visible without JavaScript and respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. `PlanTracker` saves ticked tasks on the device and exports an `.ics` file. That matches most of what the evidence asks for, and it is more than any free competitor offers.

The gaps are in what happens after the reveal and on the next visit. The self-check is a binary "Mark as practised", so the site never learns which questions went badly and cannot bring them back. Harkin's recorded-progress effect, Rowland's feedback effect and spacing all depend on that missing signal. The journey rail marks a chapter "seen" when it scrolls past. That is page-visit progress, which Khan explicitly moved away from in favour of per-skill states. The AI PM plans block question types by day (product sense on day 2, metrics on day 4) and never revisit a question, when the evidence favours mixed sets and spaced returns to the same items. The calendar export hard-codes "45 minutes a day at 8 pm, starting tomorrow". That skips the learner's own if-then choice, which is the active ingredient in implementation intentions, and it sets a larger minimum than Duolingo's one-lesson lesson suggests. Questions have no source, date, round or company tags. So they cannot answer the accuracy complaints, feed filters, or stand as their own pages. Finally, the dedicated question bank at `/interview-questions/[role]` covers generic roles (software engineer, product manager and others) but none of the six hub roles. The site's most differentiated questions therefore exist only as accordions inside long pages.

### The first eight items close the practice loop

Items 1 to 4 belong together, because each needs the others. A per-question rating (item 1) is the signal the review queue (item 2) runs on. The queue gives the plan generator (item 3) something to schedule beyond fresh reading, and the skill-based counter (item 4) makes the result visible. Together they replace three weak forms of progress (scroll-seen, ticked, practised) with one strong one: practised, rated and due. For the plan generator, take the existing 7/14/30-day tasks and change three things. Let the interview date set the length and the gaps. After a blocked first exposure to each question type, schedule mixed sets in which yesterday's weak questions come back. And let the learner write the if-then line ("Tuesday, 8 pm, at my desk") before the `.ics` file downloads. Keep "1 question" as a legitimate day. Duolingo's goal-streak split showed that ambitious self-chosen targets suppress the behaviour they are meant to drive.

Items 5 and 6 are editorial work, and they are where the site gains information that competitors lack. Every competitor page studied either has no answers (IGotAnOffer's AI PM guide lists 19 questions with none ([IGotAnOffer](https://igotanoffer.com/en/advice/ai-product-manager-interview))) or keeps them behind a paywall. A labelled weak, good and strong contrast for one question per type teaches the "listening for" criteria better than a single model answer. It also puts the site on the right side of the anti-template complaint. Provenance fields (round, company type, source, month reported) cost one data-model change in `data/roleHubs/types.ts`, and they are a precondition for items 9 to 11.

Item 7 replaces an idea the competitor research favoured, a free AI mock interview, with one that fits the no-AI-API constraint. A timed, interleaved self-mock with local recording and rubric self-scoring keeps the ingredients the evidence actually supports: content-matched questions, a planning pause, speaking aloud, criteria-based feedback and mixed types. It drops only automated scoring, which no study here validates. The existing "Say it out loud in a mock interview" link can stay, but the in-page self-mock should be the default next step, so the loop never leaves the page. Item 8 is cheap. It needs two or three cold questions per round section and a sentence explaining why getting them wrong helps, because Pan and Rivers found that learners will not trust the method otherwise.

### The design and performance items protect what search already rewards

Items 12 to 14 are not learning features, but they decide whether learners stay long enough to reach the learning features. NN/g's eye-tracking shows attention concentrated in the first two screens ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/)). Google's click systems, per DOJ trial testimony, keep about 13 months of data on what searchers click and engage with ([Marie Haynes on NavBoost](https://www.mariehaynes.com/navboost/)), and leak analyses distinguish "good clicks" from quick returns to the results page ([Resoneo](https://www.resoneo.com/google-leak-part-5-click-data-navboost-glue-and-beyond-google-is-watching-you/)). The goal is a fast, satisfying answer and an obvious next step, not inflated time on page. Keep what the site already does right. Collapsed answers count fully for ranking when they are in the HTML ([Search Engine Roundtable, Illyes](https://seroundtable.com/google-index-rank-content-in-accordions-tabs-fully-26379.html)), and content stays visible when JavaScript or motion is off ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/prefers-reduced-motion)). Any new feature from items 1 to 8 must follow the same rule. Text ships in the server HTML, and the client adds only state.

## 1,000 clicks a day needs a page portfolio, not six better hubs

The traffic target matters for planning. Semrush estimates Exponent's organic search traffic at **19.71K visits a month, about 650 a day**, and most of its top keywords are brand terms ([Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/website/tryexponent.com/overview/)). These are third-party estimates, not measured traffic. Still, reaching 1,000 non-brand clicks a day means outperforming the category leader's estimated organic footprint, and six URLs cannot capture enough distinct queries to do that, however good they are. Passage ranking lets one long page rank for narrow sub-queries, but the title, H1 and snippet are shared ([Google ranking systems guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ranking-systems-guide)). Google says AI Overviews and AI Mode fan a query out into sub-searches ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)). Ahrefs found that by March 2026 **only about 38% of AI Overview citations came from the top 10**, down from about 76% in July 2025, though Ahrefs notes its parsing method changed ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-citations-top-10/)). Citations increasingly go to pages that answer the sub-questions.

The SERPs for these roles are soft. In a non-Google search index used as a stand-in, "forward deployed engineer interview" returned mostly Glassdoor country-mirror search pages. "Chief of staff interview questions" returned almost entirely employer-side templates (Indeed, Gusto, Jotform, Yardstick), and "founder's office interview questions" returned templated Board Infinity pages with no sources or pay data ([Board Infinity](https://www.boardinfinity.com/blog/sanfe-founders-office-interview-preparation-guide/); [Umbrex](https://umbrex.com/resources/chief-of-staff-handbook/common-interview-questions-and-model-answers/)). GTM engineer candidate content is mostly hiring guides plus 13 Taro experience reports for Clay ([Taro](https://www.jointaro.com/interviews/companies/clay/experiences/gtm-engineer-new-york-ny-july-9-2025-accepted-offer-neutral-e51d0d3e)). India shows up in prep-site demand. It is 12.3% of Exponent's audience and the top country for Hello Interview ([Semrush](https://www.semrush.com/website/tryexponent.com/overview/); [Similarweb](https://www.similarweb.com/website/tryexponent.com/competitors)). Job listings advertise founder's office roles at "upto 35 LPA" ([Weekday](https://jobs.weekday.works/founder-s-office-upto-35-lpa-at-coderound-ai-4404684875)).

Those facts suggest a portfolio built in layers. The six hubs stay as the authoritative entry points. Under each sits a question bank page (item 9) and deep pages for the questions that have real search demand (item 11). Both inherit the reveal, self-check and review loop, so each new page is also a practice surface. Company × role pages (item 10) should go only where the site holds real loop data: rounds, take-homes, and dated questions from user reports and public posts. Board Infinity's templated pages show the failure mode, and Google's people-first guidance warns against pages "mainly summarizing what others have to say" ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)). The no-login "tell us your loop" form from item 5 is the supply line for those pages, as Taro's reports are for its own. Comparison pages (item 20), the GitHub repo and cheat sheets (item 19), and the shareable card (item 18) carry less weight for clicks but are the most likely to earn links and mentions. Structure every new page the same way. Use one H1, sequential question-shaped H2s with a direct answer first, real lists and tables, and schema that mirrors visible text. AirOps found these traits over-represented among ChatGPT-cited pages, as a correlation and not proof of cause ([AirOps](https://www.airops.com/report/structuring-content-for-llms)).

## What to avoid

The clearest warnings come from evidence of harm or of null results. Do not build a daily streak that resets silently. Barasch and Silverman found that people who break a streak often quit the activity, and that "get back on track" messages typically fail ([CU Boulder, JCR 2023](https://www.colorado.edu/business/news/2023/04/20/research-streaks-marketing-tech-barasch)). Do not lean on generic "make a plan" prompts. At the scale of about 250,000 MOOC students, light planning nudges had effects "an order of magnitude smaller" than small studies reported, and the effects faded ([ScienceDaily/Cornell, PNAS 2020](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2020/06/200615152116.htm)). Concrete, role-specific next actions are the better bet. Do not add urgency or scarcity to free practice: countdowns, "3 spots left" or fake timers all fall under the FTC's false-belief category. Do not label the completion card a "certificate" that could be mistaken for a verified credential ([NLR on FTC report](https://natlawreview.com/article/ftc-releases-report-dark-patterns)). Do not show mobile pop-ups on search landing pages, and do not ask for push permission on first visit ([Smashing](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2017/05/intrusive-interstitials-guidelines-avoid-google-penalty/); [Chromium](https://blog.chromium.org/2020/01/introducing-quieter-permission-ui-for.html)). Do not send guilt-based or repeated reminder copy. Duolingo found repeated templates lose effect, and it caps notification volume to "protect the channel" ([Yancey & Settles](https://research.Duolingo.com/papers/yancey.kdd20.pdf); [Lenny's Newsletter](https://lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)).

Several other patterns would quietly undermine the design. Do not use "pick the best explanation" multiple choice for self-checks, because it is the weakest self-explanation format. Do not force the attempt step with no skip, because expertise reversal means advanced readers lose out, and forced steps likely cause abandonment. Do not write ego-focused feedback ("great job", "you're not ready"). Feedback should be task-focused ("your answer didn't state a success metric"). Do not build leaderboards or comments, which need identity and moderation that conflict with no-login. On the technical side, never render content as `opacity: 0` on the server while waiting for an observer. Never fetch answers on click, since Googlebot does not interact with pages ([Google lazy-loading guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/javascript/lazy-loading)). Never apply `data-nosnippet` to wrapper components. Do not pad pages to a word count, because Google says it has no preferred length ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)). Keep "8-second attention span" and "learning styles" claims out of the copy ([Pashler et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1539-6053.2009.01038.x)). And do not mass-produce company × role pages without real loop data.

## What remains unproven

The biggest uncertainty is transfer. Retrieval, spacing, interleaving, pretesting and worked-example findings come from maths, science, texts and word pairs. No study found here tests them on spoken, open-ended interview answers. Even in real STEM courses, spaced quizzing helped significantly in only 2 of 9 courses ([Bego et al. 2024](https://doaj.org/article/13125faeb142423695a0e121ef8777ef)). Interview coaching has positive but small, mostly older evidence, with no modern meta-analysis. One study found that more professional preparation was associated with _lower_ self-reported success, though it relied on self-report and unequal groups ([Schudlik et al. 2020](https://core.ac.uk/download/355872343.pdf)). No published data was found on the effect of read-time labels, progress bars on free content sites, reveal-the-answer formats in commercial products, `.ics` calendar exports, printable checklists, WhatsApp Channel engagement for education, or shareable cards as a source of backlinks. Duolingo's strongest streak numbers are correlational (7-day streak holders were 3.6x more likely to finish a course), and its ex-CPO warns of selection bias. Whether streak-style mechanics work at all without identity, when clearing the browser or switching devices wipes them, is untested.

The SEO side has its own gaps. The SERP observations come from a non-Google index. No keyword volumes were obtained for FDE, GTM engineer, founder's office, AI PM or chief of staff queries. Nobody has verified whether third-party LLM crawlers cite collapsed accordion content as often as visible text. India's current CrUX pass rates, device mix and per-GB data prices were not retrieved. The most recent verified device data is from 2019. The legal reading that localStorage progress which never leaves the device is not "processing" under India's DPDP Act or GDPR is an inference that needs counsel. Each of these is testable on the site itself. Search Console, CrUX and simple local counters (attempts, ratings, return visits per device) will show within weeks which of the twenty changes earn their place.

## Conclusion

The site's real competitor is not Exponent's page layout. It is the learner's habit of reading an answer, feeling ready and leaving. Every evidence-backed change here does one thing: it turns reading into a recorded attempt and gives that attempt a date to come back. Because the site already folds answers behind an attempt in server-rendered HTML, the missing pieces are mostly data. A rating per question, a due date per question and an interview date per learner turn the existing page into a spaced, mixed practice system that no free competitor runs and that paid competitors charge for.

The traffic goal changes what kind of site this needs to be. Six hubs can be the best on the internet and still fall short of 1,000 clicks a day. The question-level loop that improves learning also creates the pages that rank: a dated, sourced, rated question with a contrast answer can be a passage in a hub, a row in a filterable bank and its own page cited in an AI Overview. Build the data model once, with round, company type, source, date, criteria and contrasts, and the learner experience and the page portfolio grow from the same source.
