## What is the average SDR ramp time in 2026?

**The best current figure is 3.0 months, from The Bridge Group's 2025 SDR report (351 B2B companies, surveyed 2024–25).** It is the lowest the study has recorded since 2010; the peak was 3.8 months in 2014. The same report puts average SDR tenure at 1.9 years, the median quota at 10 "Stage 0" meetings held a month, and the share of SDRs at quota at 60%, the lowest in its history ([2025 report](https://www.bridgegroupinc.com/research/2025-sdr-models-metrics-report-the-bridge-group)).

Two caveats matter before you compare your team with it. The report does not define when ramp ends, and its sample leans to North American SaaS (78% North America, 83% B2B SaaS). So use 3.0 months as a reference point, then measure your own (see [how to calculate ramp time](#how-do-you-calculate-ramp-time)).

### How long does an AE take to ramp?

**Account executives took 6.2 months in 2026, the longest in the Bridge Group study's history, across 158 B2B companies.** Only 48% of AEs were at quota, down from 51% in 2024. Jason Lemkin of SaaStr gives a rule of thumb by segment: an SMB AE should be "ramped to quota by the second" sales cycle, mid-market takes "5-6 months to truly scale-up", and enterprise "typically 6-9 months to fully ramp" ([SaaStr, Oct 2025](https://www.saastr.com/dear-saastr-how-long-should-new-account-execs-get-to-make-quota)).

### What makes ramp longer?

**Deal size and sales cycle matter most: the longer a buyer takes to decide, the longer a new rep takes to prove they can close.** Lemkin's rule of thumb for AEs runs from the second sales cycle in SMB to 6–9 months in enterprise. For SDRs, the same pressures show up as a harder product to explain, a narrower list of accounts, and outbound-only targets. Put your own ramp months in the calculator rather than borrowing the average.

## What does a ramp cost?

**On an even ramp, an SDR's output over an n-month ramp falls n ÷ 2 months short of full quota, so a 3-month ramp costs 1.5 months of pay with no matching output.** For a median US SDR (\$80,000 on-target pay plus employer benefits, \$114,286 a year) that is \$14,286 and 15 held meetings below full quota:

Three inputs decide most answers:

- **Quota in held meetings.** Use held, not booked. The Bridge Group median is 10 a month, but only 60% of SDRs reach it.
- **Tenure.** At 1.9 years, a 3-month ramp eats about 7% of an average SDR's whole stay. If your tenure is shorter, ramp matters more.

### What is a ramped quota?

**A ramped quota is a reduced target for a new rep's first months, rising to full quota when ramp ends.** QuotaPath's 2025 survey of 114 revenue leaders found six months the most common ramp period; as an example schedule it gives "Month 1: 25% of full quota / Month 2: 50% / Month 3: 75% / Month 4+: 100%", and quarterly plans that ramp "50% > 75% > 100%" ([QuotaPath](https://www.quotapath.com/blog/sales-ramping-comp-plans/)). GitLab's SDR handbook uses 25/50/75/100% over months 1–4 and starts the clock in the following month when a rep joins on or after the 15th ([GitLab](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/marketing/sales-development/sdr-playbook-onboarding/)).

## The 90-day SDR onboarding plan

**The plan below spends the first two weeks on scored practice calls instead of passive shadowing, gates live dials on a passed certification in week three, and runs GitLab's 25/50/75/100% ramp with month 1 counting the onboarding weeks.** Adjust the counts to your product and to the time your managers have to review sessions.

<SdrRampPlanFlow />

### Weeks 1–2: learn the buyer, then practise out loud

- **Days 1–3:** the ideal customer profile, the three pains you solve, competitors, and the tools (CRM, dialer, sequencer). Listen to five recorded calls: two good, two bad, one lost.
- **Days 4–10:** 8–10 scored practice calls a day against an AI buyer or a colleague, on four moments: the opener, the gatekeeper, "not interested", and "send me an email". Review two of them with the manager each day.
- **Shadow on purpose:** three or four live calls, each with one thing to listen for. Shadowing shows real buyers; it is not where the rep practises.

### Week 3: the certification gate

- Three scored practice calls, one per moment the rep will meet first, each picked at random.
- **Set the bar before the week starts** (for example, every rubric line at "meets") and write it down. If the rep misses, they practise the weak moment and retake it; nobody dials live leads before passing.
- Forrester's Peter Ostrow found that only "7% of companies put them in front of buyers on day one" ([Forrester, May 2024](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/how-quickly-should-a-sales-rep-be-onboarded)). A gate makes that decision explicit instead of a matter of calendar.

### Days 22–120: live calls on a ramped quota

- **Month 1 (days 1–30, live from day 22):** 25% of quota. Each morning starts with a 10-minute warm-up roleplay on the moment the rep lost most often the day before.
- **Month 2:** 50%. Weekly review of two recorded live calls against the same rubric used in practice.
- **Month 3:** 75%. The rep runs one practice session a week for a newer hire, which forces them to explain the talk track.
- **Month 4:** full quota.

### When do you know an SDR isn't working out?

**Lemkin's answer is about three months, with an earlier signal: "You'll probably know in 30 days"** ([SaaStr, Aug 2023](https://www.saastr.com/how-long-do-you-have-as-an-sdr-bdr-if-you-arent-hitting-your-quota)). Use the gate as your 30-day read: a rep who cannot pass a scored practice call by week four needs a coaching plan, not more live dials.

## How do you calculate ramp time?

**Ramp time is the months from a rep's start date to the first month they hit full quota.** Pick one definition and keep it for every cohort. Track these four alongside it:

- **Time to first live call** and **time to first held meeting** after the start date.
- **Practice calls per rep per week** in weeks 1–3, from your roleplay tool or a simple log.
- **Certification pass rate** at the week-three gate, and how many attempts it took.
- **Months at or above ramped quota** in months 1–3.

Compare the next cohort with the last two. A cohort of 3–5 hires is small, so read the result as a direction, not a percentage. That applies to every "X% faster ramp" claim you will see from vendors, including us: none we found cites an independent study.

## Why practice calls instead of shadowing?

**Shadowing shows a new rep real buyers; practice is where they learn to say the words under pressure, and most teams don't offer enough of it.** In Salesforce's 2026 report, 41% of reps said they don't get enough chances to roleplay before customer calls and 46% "rarely get feedback on my sales conversations"; only 26% of teams list roleplay sessions among the enablement they offer ([State of Sales PDF](https://www.salesforce.com/en-us/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/documents/reports/sales/salesforce-state-of-sales-report-2026.pdf)). Salesforce's own news story about the report gives 47% for the first figure ([announcement](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/state-of-sales-report-announcement-2026/)); we use the report's 41%.

|                        | Call shadowing                      | Trainer workshop             | Scored practice calls                  |
| :--------------------- | :---------------------------------- | :--------------------------- | :------------------------------------- |
| **The new hire**       | Listens to senior reps              | Learns a method in a group   | Speaks on many calls alone             |
| **Feedback**           | Informal, when someone has time     | From the trainer, on the day | A score against your rubric every call |
| **Progress visible?**  | Only if the manager listens         | To the trainer, on the day   | To the manager, for every session      |
| **Risk to real leads** | High: first live calls are practice | Medium                       | Lower: live calls start after a gate   |
| **Cost**               | Senior reps' and managers' time     | A day rate per workshop      | A subscription plus review time        |
| **Best for**           | Hearing real buyers in context      | Teaching a new sales method  | Repetition until the talk track sticks |

Most teams use all three: a workshop for the method, a few shadowed calls for context, practice calls for the repetitions. ## Onboarding remote and hybrid SDRs

**Remote onboarding needs the same plan with three things made explicit: a daily schedule, recorded practice the manager can review later, and a named buddy.** In an office, a new SDR overhears good calls all day; at home they hear nothing unless you schedule it.

- **Fixed daily blocks:** a 15-minute stand-up, two practice blocks, and a listening block with recorded calls.
- **Asynchronous review:** practice calls recorded and scored, so a manager in another time zone can review them the next morning.
- **A buddy:** one ramped SDR who joins a call a day in weeks 1–2 and answers questions in chat.
- **India teams selling to the US or UK:** schedule the practice block in the buyer's morning, and practise the time-zone moments ("can we do 9 p.m. your time?") as their own scenario.

## What to do if you are...

- **A founder hiring your first SDR:** budget for 3 months of reduced output and set the ramp quota in writing before the start date. Use "nothing in month 1" if the product needs real training.
- **An SDR manager with a new cohort:** run the week-three gate, measure the four numbers above, and compare with your last two cohorts before changing anything else.
- **RevOps writing the comp plan:** paste the calculator's schedule into the plan, and decide the mid-month start rule (GitLab's is the 15th).
- **An SDR in your first month:** ask for the rubric on day one and practise the four moments out loud every day. Our [free cold-call practice simulator](/cold-call-practice) is a place to start.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it take a new SDR to ramp?

About 3 months on average: The Bridge Group's 2025 survey of 351 B2B companies found 3.0 months, down from a 3.8-month peak in 2014. Your number depends on deal size, product complexity and how you define the end of ramp, so measure it per cohort.

### What is a good ramp time for an SDR?

At or under 3 months is in line with the market average. If your SDRs take much longer, check the ramp definition first, then whether new hires get enough scored practice before live calls.

### What quota should an SDR have during ramp?

A common schedule is 25% of full quota in month 1, 50% in month 2, 75% in month 3 and 100% from month 4, which is what GitLab publishes for its SDRs. Teams with longer training use 0% in month 1.

### What is the difference between onboarding and ramp time?

Onboarding is the training programme; in the plan above it is the first three weeks. Ramp time runs from the start date until the rep first hits full quota, so it includes onboarding plus the months on a ramped quota.

### How can you reduce SDR ramp time?

Replace most shadowing with scored practice calls, gate live dials on a passed certification, keep a short daily warm-up after the gate, and review live calls weekly against the same rubric. Then measure the next cohort against the last two; no vendor figure replaces your own.

### Does AI roleplay shorten ramp time?

We found no independent study that measures it. AI roleplay makes more practice possible without a manager in every session, which is the gap Salesforce's survey points to. Whether it shortens your ramp is something to measure on your own hires.
