If you are building an AI calling system in India, you will eventually land on the same two names: Plivo and Vobiz. Plivo is the global incumbent with a decade of infrastructure behind it. Vobiz is the India-native upstart built specifically for AI voice workloads. I have run production systems on both. This is not a marketing comparison. This is what you actually need to know before you pick one.

The short answer: **Plivo at ₹0.60/min with ₹250/month numbers wins at low volume. Vobiz at ₹0.45/min with ₹600+/month numbers wins at scale.** The break-even is exactly 2,333 minutes per month per number. But that math misses the more important variable, which I will get to.

## What These Two Companies Actually Are

Plivo started in 2011 in San Francisco. It is a global CPaaS platform that does voice, SMS, WhatsApp, and more across 190+ countries. India is one market among many for them. Their SIP trunking product is called Zentrunk. Clients include Meta, Zomato, Razorpay, and Uber in India. It is a mature, well-documented platform.

Vobiz launched in 2024 out of Bengaluru. They built one product: developer-friendly telephony for AI voice agents in India. They have an MCP server so LLMs can manage calls as tool calls. They wrote an `llms.txt` discovery file. Their docs are on Mintlify. They are AI-native in a way Plivo is not.

The philosophical difference matters: Plivo was built for developers adding voice to apps. Vobiz was built for AI agents making calls autonomously.

## The Factor Everyone Ignores: Number Series Pickup Rates

This is the insight that separates people who have actually run AI calling in India from people who have not.

The pickup rate on outbound AI calls in India varies by a factor of 4 to 5 depending on what number series you call from. Not by 10-20%. By a factor of four.

Here is why: When an unknown number calls an Indian mobile user, the first thing they see is the number format. A 140-series number is immediately recognized as a commercial/promotional call. Most people have been conditioned to ignore these. A 7972 or 92 series number looks like a personal mobile number. The prospect does not know it is commercial before answering.

Estimated pickup rates by series:

| Number Series      | What It Looks Like    | Pickup Rate | Provider     |
| :----------------- | :-------------------- | :---------- | :----------- |
| 140 series         | TRAI commercial code  | 8-12%       | Plivo, Vobiz |
| 080 / 020 landline | City STD code         | 15-22%      | Plivo, Vobiz |
| 7972 series        | Mobile format         | 30-38%      | Vobiz only   |
| 92 series          | Premium mobile format | 38-48%      | Vobiz only   |

Plivo gives you 080, 020, and 140. Vobiz gives you 7972, 92, 140, 080, and 160 (BFSI-only).

If you are doing outbound consumer calling, the 92-series number is roughly 4x more effective than a 140-series number from the same call list. That means your AI agent needs to make four times fewer calls to achieve the same number of conversations. The ₹650 monthly premium on the number pays for itself after approximately five additional meetings.

The per-minute rate difference between Plivo and Vobiz is ₹0.15. The conversation volume difference from number series is 400%. Focus on the bigger variable.

## Indian Number Series: What Each One Is For

**080 / 020 (STD landline codes)**: These are Bengaluru and Pune codes respectively. They read as professional business numbers in South India and Maharashtra. Good for B2B outreach where a landline origin is trustworthy. Both Plivo and Vobiz offer these.

**140 series**: TRAI's designated series for commercial voice outreach. Before you can use these for AI calling, you must register your entity on the DLT (Distributed Ledger Technology) platform run by Indian carriers. The registration takes three to five working days. Once approved, you get a Principal Entity ID, then register each campaign/script template. Without this, carriers auto-block your calls at the network level. This trips up a lot of first-time operators.

**7972 series (Vobiz)**: Mobile-format number that does not immediately signal commercial intent. Significantly higher pickup rates than landline or 140-series. The number looks personal until the prospect answers and hears your AI agent.

**92 series (Vobiz)**: The highest-pickup series in Vobiz's inventory. The 92 prefix is strongly associated with standard consumer mobile numbers in India. Answer rates approach 40-48% in practice. These numbers cost more per month but the conversion math usually makes them the highest-ROI choice for consumer outreach.

**160 series (Vobiz only)**: Exclusively for BFSI entities making service or transactional calls (not promotional). Banks, NBFCs, and insurance companies running AI calling for account updates, policy renewals, or loan reminders need this series. Requires SEBI or RBI entity documentation to provision. Plivo does not offer this series.

## TRAI Compliance: What Will Get You Blocked

Both providers are TRAI-compliant at the infrastructure level. But compliance is your responsibility as the operator, not theirs. Here is what will get your numbers blocked if you skip it.

**DLT registration for 140-series**: This is the non-negotiable one. TRAI's DLT platform requires you to register your entity (three to five days), register each outbound campaign, and link your DID to the registration. Carriers actively check this and block unregistered commercial calls. I have seen companies spend days debugging why their AI calls were failing before realizing they had the infrastructure right but the DLT registration wrong.

**Calling hours**: No commercial calls before 9 AM or after 9 PM in the prospect's local time zone. Build this into your scheduling logic, not your AI agent. The SIP provider will route the call regardless of hour. The restriction is regulatory.

**DNC scrubbing**: Both Plivo and Vobiz scrub against the National Do Not Call Registry automatically. But you must also honor verbal opt-outs during calls (update your list within 24 hours) and retain call records for 90 days for audit purposes.

**AI disclosure**: India does not have a formal AI disclosure mandate yet, but TRAI issued a consultation paper in 2025 signaling it is coming. Starting calls with "I am an AI assistant calling on behalf of [Company]" is currently best practice and will soon likely be mandatory.

## Technical Architecture: How They Connect to Your AI Stack

Both Plivo and Vobiz integrate with the major AI voice platforms. The connection model differs slightly.

With **Plivo + LiveKit**: You configure a Plivo Zentrunk SIP credential, bind it to a LiveKit SIP service, and LiveKit bridges the audio between the PSTN call and your AI agent's room. Plivo handles carrier routing; LiveKit handles audio processing and AI model communication.

```python
# Plivo SIP trunk binding in LiveKit (simplified)
# Full guide: plivo.com/docs/sip-trunking/guides/ai-voice-agents

import os
from livekit import api

async def start_outbound_call(to_number: str):
    lk = api.LiveKitAPI(
        url=os.environ["LIVEKIT_URL"],
        api_key=os.environ["LIVEKIT_API_KEY"],
        api_secret=os.environ["LIVEKIT_API_SECRET"],
    )
    # Plivo routes India calls to ap-south-1 (Mumbai) automatically
    # Enable region pinning in Plivo console for TRAI compliance
    await lk.sip.create_sip_outbound_trunk(
        trunk_uri="sip:your-trunk@sip.plivo.com",
        from_number="+918069XXXXXX",   # Your Plivo 080-series DID
        to_number=to_number,
        room_name="ai-call-session",
    )
```

With **Vobiz**: You can go directly to WebSocket audio streaming without an intermediary. Vobiz accepts a WebSocket URL and streams raw PCM audio bidirectionally. This eliminates one hop in the signal chain.

```python
# Vobiz native WebSocket call initiation
# Full guide: vobiz.ai/docs/introduction

import aiohttp, os

async def start_vobiz_call(to_number: str, from_number: str):
    async with aiohttp.ClientSession() as session:
        async with session.post(
            "https://api.vobiz.ai/v1/calls",
            json={
                "from": from_number,        # Your Vobiz 7972 or 92-series DID
                "to": to_number,
                "streaming": {
                    "url": "wss://your-ai-agent.com/audio",
                    "codec": "pcm_16000",  # 16kHz PCM for OpenAI/Deepgram
                },
            },
            headers={"Authorization": f"Bearer {os.environ['VOBIZ_API_KEY']}"},
        ) as response:
            data = await response.json()
            return data["call_sid"]
```

One Vobiz capability worth mentioning for teams building fully autonomous systems: they maintain an MCP (Model Context Protocol) server at `vobiz.ai/docs/mcp`. This means an LLM can initiate and manage calls as native tool calls without you writing custom API wrappers. If you are building agentic systems where the AI manages its own outreach pipeline, this matters.

## How the SIP Network Fits Into Your Total Latency

A mistake I see often: treating SIP network latency as the primary latency problem in AI calling.

Plivo and Vobiz both deliver around 80-100ms of network latency for India-to-India calls. That is fast. The issue is that for most AI calling platforms, network latency is the smallest piece of the response time.

A standard cascade-architecture AI agent (Speech-to-Text, then LLM, then Text-to-Speech) adds 800-1200ms of processing time on top of whatever the network contributes. Total response delay: over one second. Indian mobile users notice this. They interpret it as the call dropping or the agent malfunctioning. This is a meaningful driver of early hang-ups.

The 20ms network latency difference between Plivo and Vobiz matters far less than whether your AI voice engine uses a cascade pipeline or processes audio natively. Native voice-to-voice engines like TTGE respond in under 200ms total end-to-end, including network. At that latency level, the conversation feels like talking to a person. At 1200ms, it does not.

If your SIP provider choice is Vobiz but your AI engine is a cascade pipeline, you will have better Indian number pickup rates and a lower per-minute bill, and your calls will still feel robotic. Fix the voice engine first, then optimize the SIP layer.

## How to Choose

There is no universally correct answer. Here is how I think through it:

**Choose Plivo if** you are building an MVP or proof of concept and need to make calls in India within an hour. Plivo's documentation for AI platform integrations (LiveKit, Vapi, Retell) is comprehensive, their onboarding is frictionless, and their number cost is low enough that you are not burning money during validation. If you also need to call internationally across 190+ countries, Plivo's global coverage is unmatched.

**Choose Vobiz if** you have validated your use case and are optimizing for scale. Above 2,333 minutes per month per number, the per-minute savings compound. The 7972 and 92-series numbers materially improve your conversation rates on Indian consumer outreach. The WebSocket audio streaming and MCP server are advantages if you are building an AI-native infrastructure stack rather than routing through an intermediary. The GST ITC benefit is real for Indian entities.

**Run both if** you are A/B testing number series pickup rates, or if you want geographic redundancy. Plivo for B2B or international calls, Vobiz for high-volume Indian consumer outreach.

The most common trajectory I see: start on Plivo, hit 3-5x call volume growth, realize the Vobiz math makes sense, migrate the high-volume Indian consumer campaigns to Vobiz while keeping Plivo for everything else.

## What No One Tells You About Operating AI Calling in India at Scale

Three things I wish I had known earlier:

**First**: The DLT registration process for 140-series numbers is slower than it sounds. Three to five business days is the official timeline. In practice, if your documentation has any issues, it can take two weeks. If you are planning a campaign launch with a hard date, start the DLT registration three weeks before, not three days.

**Second**: Number reputation degrades over time. If your AI agent gets flagged too many times (unanswered calls that get reported as spam, poor call quality, high early hang-up rates), carriers start silently downranking your number. You will see answer rates drop even with the same number series. The solution is call quality improvement and number rotation, not switching SIP providers.

**Third**: The conversation-to-outcome rate of your AI agent is usually more important than the cost per minute. A ₹0.15/min savings matters. A 10% improvement in your AI agent's conversation quality matters ten times more. Invest in your voice model and conversation design before you spend time optimizing infrastructure costs.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which SIP provider is better for AI calling in India, Plivo or Vobiz?

Vobiz is better for high-volume Indian AI calling above 2,333 minutes per month per number, primarily because of its lower per-minute rate (₹0.45 vs ₹0.60) and access to mobile-format number series (7972, 92) that achieve 30-48% pickup rates on outbound calls. Plivo is better for starting quickly, validating a use case, or calling internationally across 190+ countries where Vobiz's 130-country coverage is insufficient.

### What is Plivo's per-minute rate for SIP trunking in India?

Plivo charges ₹0.60 per minute for both inbound and outbound SIP trunking calls in India as of August 2026, with virtual numbers (DIDs) at ₹250 per month. Pricing is pay-as-you-go with no minimum spend, no contracts, and per-second billing. Source: [plivo.com/sip-trunking/pricing](https://www.plivo.com/sip-trunking/pricing/)

### What is Vobiz's per-minute rate for SIP trunking in India?

Vobiz charges ₹0.45 per minute for both inbound and outbound calls in India as of August 2026, with virtual numbers starting at ₹600 per month for standard series and higher for premium 92-series numbers. Source: [vobiz.ai/pricing](https://vobiz.ai/pricing)

### What Indian number series does Vobiz offer that Plivo does not?

Vobiz offers 7972-series mobile-format numbers (30-38% pickup rate), 92-series premium mobile-format numbers (38-48% pickup rate), and 160-series BFSI-exclusive numbers for banks, NBFCs, and insurance companies making transactional calls. Plivo offers 080, 020, and 140 series only. The mobile-format number series are Vobiz's strongest competitive advantage for Indian consumer outreach.

### Do I need DLT registration to use AI calling in India?

Yes, if you are using 140-series commercial numbers for outbound calls. TRAI requires all commercial voice calling entities to register on the DLT (Distributed Ledger Technology) platform, obtain a Principal Entity ID, and register each campaign before calls can be made. Carriers automatically block 140-series calls from unregistered entities. The registration process takes three to five business days under normal conditions. Both Plivo and Vobiz support DLT-compliant infrastructure, but registration itself is the operator's responsibility.

### Is Vobiz TRAI-compliant for AI calling in India?

Yes. Vobiz is fully TRAI-compliant with KYC verification for number provisioning, DNC registry scrubbing for outbound calls, and support for DLT registration requirements. Their infrastructure meets TRAI's media localization requirements for Indian traffic processing.

### Can I use Plivo and Vobiz at the same time?

Yes. Many production AI calling operations use both providers simultaneously: Plivo for international or low-volume Indian calls and Vobiz for high-volume Indian consumer outbound where mobile-format numbers and lower per-minute rates matter most. Running both also provides redundancy if one provider has an outage.

### What is the latency for AI voice calling in India with these providers?

Plivo and Vobiz both deliver approximately 80-100ms of network latency for India-to-India calls. This is not the dominant latency variable in AI voice calling. The bigger factor is your AI voice engine: cascade-architecture systems (STT + LLM + TTS) add 800-1200ms of processing time per conversational turn, resulting in noticeable response delays that drive early hang-ups. Native voice-to-voice engines process audio end-to-end in under 200ms. Choose your voice engine architecture before optimizing between SIP providers.
