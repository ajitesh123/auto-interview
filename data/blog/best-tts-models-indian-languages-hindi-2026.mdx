## Why Standard Global TTS Fails in the Indian Market

Global speech synthesis engines trained on Western datasets encounter severe breakdown when processing conversational Indian text. Indian voice AI deployments require handling three distinct linguistic traits:

1. **Intra-Sentence Code-Switching (Hinglish):** In commercial calls, users speak Hindi mixed with English business terms (e.g., _"Aapka loan approval letter humne WhatsApp pe share kar diya hai, please check the interest rate."_). Standard models either mispronounce English words with exaggerated Hindi phonetics or treat English words as syntax errors.
2. **Retroflex Consonants & Syllable Timing:** Indic languages are syllable-timed (each syllable takes roughly equal duration), whereas English is stress-timed. American TTS models applied to Hindi sound robotic and erratic because they apply Western stress patterns to Indian phonemes.
3. **Telephony Downsampling (8,000 Hz):** Cellular calls across Indian carriers (Jio, Airtel, Vi) stream over low-bandwidth G.711u codecs. Studio-grade 48kHz audio downsampled without proper acoustic filtering sounds tinny and metallic to mobile callers.

According to speech benchmarks from [Sarvam AI Research (2025)](https://sarvam.ai), models trained specifically on multi-speaker Indic audio demonstrate a 41% higher comprehension score among Indian mobile telephony callers compared to adapted Western foundational models.

## Technical Architecture: Streaming WebSocket Integration

To achieve sub-200ms latency, engineering teams must stream LLM token chunks directly into the TTS engine over persistent WebSockets, rather than waiting for complete paragraphs to generate:

```typescript
// Example: Real-time Streaming Sentence Chunking to TTS
import WebSocket from 'ws'

const ttsSocket = new WebSocket('wss://api.sarvam.ai/v1/text-to-speech/streaming')

ttsSocket.on('open', () => {
  // Send configuration frame for Indian Telephony (8kHz PCM)
  ttsSocket.send(
    JSON.stringify({
      model: 'bulbul-v4-flash',
      speaker: 'ananya',
      language_code: 'hi-IN',
      audio_format: 'pcm_8000',
      chunk_size: 160, // 20ms telephony frames
    })
  )
})

// Stream token sentences as soon as punctuation delimiter is detected
function streamTextToSpeech(textChunk: string) {
  ttsSocket.send(JSON.stringify({ text: textChunk }))
}

ttsSocket.on('message', (audioBuffer: Buffer) => {
  // Pipe raw 8kHz PCM directly to SIP RTP audio egress buffer
  rtpAudioStream.write(audioBuffer)
})
```

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which TTS model is best for mixed Hindi-English (Hinglish)?

**Sarvam Bulbul Flash** is the superior model for Hinglish. It natively recognizes Romanized Hindi words, preserves English technical terms, and alternates phonetic stress naturally without awkward pauses.

### What is the maximum acceptable TTS latency for conversational voice agents?

For real-time phone calls, Time-to-First-Audio (TTFA) must remain **under 250 milliseconds**. When combined with 150ms for Speech-to-Text and 150ms for LLM reasoning, total conversational turnaround stays within the human threshold of 500ms.

### Can I self-host an Indian TTS model for data privacy?

Yes. **AI4Bharat Indic-TTS** (developed by IIT Madras) provides open-source weights across 22 Indian languages that can be self-hosted on an AWS EC2 instance with an NVIDIA T4 or A10G GPU.

### How does Sarvam AI compare to ElevenLabs on pricing?

Sarvam AI costs approximately **₹140 per 1M characters (~$1.68 USD)**, whereas ElevenLabs Flash v2.5 costs **$50.00 per 1M characters**. Sarvam is roughly 29x more cost-effective for high-volume automated outbound calling.
