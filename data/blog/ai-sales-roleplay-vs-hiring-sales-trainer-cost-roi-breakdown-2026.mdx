## The Ebbinghaus Forgetting Curve in Corporate Sales Training

In 1885, German psychologist Hermann Ebbinghaus conducted the first empirical study on memory retention. His mathematical findings remain unchallenged in modern cognitive science:

- Within **24 hours**, humans forget approximately **50% to 70%** of newly acquired theoretical information.
- Within **30 days**, that retention drops to **under 16%** unless the material is deliberately retrieved and practiced.

In sales, knowing a framework intellectually is meaningless. When a real buyer pushes back on pricing, the rep does not have time to recall a slide deck from three weeks ago. They rely entirely on **unconscious muscle memory**.

To beat the forgetting curve, reps require **spaced repetition**: short, high-frequency, low-stakes practice sessions executed 3 to 5 times every single week.

## Cost Per Repetition: The Decisive Metric

In sales development, mastery is a direct function of **volume of repetitions**.

Consider what happens during a $10,000 full-day workshop with a human trainer:

- The trainer spends 4 hours lecturing and walking through slides.
- The trainer spends 3 hours demonstrating calls and leading group debriefs.
- In the remaining 1 hour, **only 3 or 4 brave reps get to do a live mock call** in front of the room. The other 16 reps sit passively in their chairs.

Across the entire day, your team logged fewer than 10 total roleplay repetitions. At a cost of $10,000, that equals **$1,000 per simulated interaction**.

In **Tough Tongue AI**, every single rep on your team can run 4 targeted objection-handling drills every morning before dialing. Across 20 reps, that equals **400 repetitions per week, or over 20,000 repetitions per year**.

At an annual SaaS investment of $15,000, your effective cost per repetition drops to **$0.75 per drill**.
