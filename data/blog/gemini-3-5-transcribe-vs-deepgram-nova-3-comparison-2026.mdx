## The Core Architectural Conflict: Conformer-RNN-T vs Multimodal Transformer

Building a production voice pipeline in **2026** forces engineering teams to choose between two fundamentally opposed speech recognition architectures.

```text
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                 Deepgram Nova-3 Pipeline                     │
│  Raw Audio ──► Conformer Front-End ──► RNN-T Monotonic Greedy│
│  Result: Ultra-low latency, but literal verbatim emission   │
│          with zero semantic disfluency filtering.           │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│               Gemini 3.5 Transcribe Pipeline                │
│  Raw Audio ──► USM Conformer-2 ──► Autoregressive Decoder   │
│  Result: Sub-200ms streaming with native disfluency pruning,│
│          self-correction, and 85+ language cross-attention. │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Deepgram Nova-3 relies on a Conformer front-end paired with an RNN-Transducer (RNN-T) greedy search decoder. This architecture emits text tokens monotonically as frames arrive.

Monotonic decoding guarantees rapid time-to-first-token generation. However, it completely prevents the model from looking ahead to understand grammatical intent.

Google Gemini 3.5 Transcribe employs a deep Universal Speech Model (USM) Conformer-2 acoustic encoder connected to an autoregressive transformer decoder. The decoder evaluates acoustic embeddings across semantic attention heads, resolving stutters and hesitations before emitting final tokens.

## Deconstructing the FLEURS Benchmark: 5.50% vs 15.77% WER

The official August 2026 FLEURS evaluation tested both models across 25 top global locales, including `en-US`, `hi-IN`, `ar-EG`, `bn-IN`, `ja-JP`, `de-DE`, and `es-ES`.

![FLEURS Benchmark: Gemini 3.5 Transcribe Live at 5.50% WER vs Deepgram Nova-3 at 15.77%](/static/images/blog/gemini-3-5-transcribe-fleurs-benchmark.png)

### Why Deepgram Collapsed on Multilingual Audio

To understand why Deepgram Nova-3 scored **15.77% WER**, we must examine token vocabulary distributions:

1. **Sub-Word Token Vocabulary Sparsity:** Deepgram's tokenizer uses approximately **32,000 sub-word tokens** optimized for English and European morphology. When processing complex Indic scripts like Bengali (`bn-IN`) or Telugu (`te-IN`), the model lacks dedicated tokens. It fragments phonemes into individual byte-level characters, causing severe word alignment failures.
2. **Cross-Lingual Acoustic Conditioning:** Gemini 3.5 Transcribe was pre-trained on multi-million hour unlabeled audio corpuses spanning 85+ languages. Its 256,000-token multilingual vocabulary preserves root words natively, maintaining **5.50% WER** across diverse accents.
3. **SpecAugment Noise Resilience:** Over noisy mobile lines, Deepgram Nova-3 hallucinates repeated consonants during packet jitter. Gemini 3.5 Transcribe applies temporal masking during training, preventing spurious token loops.

## Disfluency Handling & Downstream LLM Token Cost

In real-world telephony, human speech is full of disfluencies: false starts, stuttered syllables, filler sounds ("um", "uh"), and mid-sentence corrections.

```text
Caller Input:
"I want to book a ticket to Mumbai... wait no, to Delhi on Friday."

Deepgram Nova-3 Output:
"I want to book a ticket to Mumbai wait no to Delhi on Friday"
(Requires downstream LLM cleanup; consumes 14 input tokens + reasoning delay)

Gemini 3.5 Transcribe Output:
"I want to book a ticket to Delhi on Friday."
(Direct structured execution; consumes 9 input tokens with zero ambiguity)
```

Deepgram transcribes every sound phonetically. When this output is sent to an LLM in a voice agent pipeline, the LLM must spend reasoning tokens interpreting the user's true intent.

Gemini 3.5 Transcribe strips filler sounds and resolves corrections directly in the acoustic decoder. This reduces prompt token overhead by **18% to 25%**, lowering total voice agent inference costs.

## Telephony Constraints: 8kHz Narrowband Performance

Standard PSTN telephone networks compress audio using G.711 μ-law codecs at **8kHz sampling rates**, cutting off all frequencies above 3.4kHz.

```text
[PSTN Carrier (8kHz G.711)] ──► [Bandwidth Limited: 300Hz - 3.4kHz]
                                             │
                       ┌─────────────────────┴─────────────────────┐
                       ▼                                           ▼
            [Deepgram Nova-3]                           [Gemini 3.5 Transcribe]
    High phonetic confusion on s/f              Conformer-2 SpecAugment defense
    and b/d unvoiced stops (WER: 12.4%)         preserves consonant stops (WER: 6.9%)
```

In 8kHz telephony conditions, high-frequency fricatives ("s", "f", "th") lose critical acoustic energy. Deepgram Nova-3 experiences phonetic confusion on unvoiced consonants, driving telephony WER to **12.4%**.

Gemini 3.5 Transcribe uses extensive data augmentation with synthetic low-pass filtering. It reconstructs missing acoustic features through contextual language priors, maintaining an **8kHz telephony WER of 6.9%**.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why did Deepgram Nova-3 score 15.77% on the FLEURS benchmark?**
Deepgram Nova-3 is optimized primarily for English conversational audio. On multilingual datasets with diverse phonetic scripts (Arabic, Hindi, Bengali), its compact sub-word vocabulary suffers from character fragmentation and high token error rates.

**Is Gemini 3.5 Transcribe faster than Deepgram Nova-3?**
For clean English streaming, Deepgram Nova-3 has a slight latency edge at **120ms to 150ms**. Gemini 3.5 Transcribe Live delivers **&lt;200ms** streaming latency while performing real-time disfluency removal and multilingual decoding.

**Can I use Gemini 3.5 Transcribe with WebSockets?**
Yes. The Live API (`gemini-3.5-transcribe-live`) operates over full-duplex WebSockets and gRPC streams, accepting 16kHz or 24kHz linear PCM audio chunks.

**Does Gemini 3.5 Transcribe support custom vocabulary?**
Yes. It supports dynamic speech context biasing, allowing developers to inject domain-specific phrase lists with probability boost multipliers up to 20.0.
