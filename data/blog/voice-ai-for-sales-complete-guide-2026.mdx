**Voice AI for sales is software that holds a spoken conversation, on the phone or in a meeting, to do a sales job. It does two different jobs: it talks to buyers for you (answering new leads, qualifying them, booking meetings), and it plays the buyer so your reps can practise. At list prices in November 2026 a voice agent costs about 7 to 31 US cents a minute. In the US an AI voice counts as an "artificial" voice under the robocall law, so you need consent before it calls anyone.**

<KeyFacts>

- **Two jobs, one technology.** An AI that calls buyers, and an AI your reps practise on. Most guides only cover the first.
- **Price:** Retell AI publishes 7 to 31 cents a minute; its own default estimate is 11 cents. A three-minute call at that rate costs 33 cents.
- **Law:** the US FCC ruled on 8 February 2024 that AI-generated voices are "artificial" under the TCPA. The EU's rule that people must be told they are talking to an AI applies from 2 August 2026.
- **Why teams want it:** in Salesforce's 2026 survey of 4,050 sales professionals, the average seller spends 40% of their time selling, and 48% say they lack the time for adequate cold outreach.

</KeyFacts>

## Which sales tasks suit voice AI today?

Our view, with the reason for each. The pattern: give the agent short, repeatable conversations where being fast matters more than being persuasive.

| Task                              | Fit today | Why                                                                           |
| --------------------------------- | --------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Answer a new inbound lead         | Strong    | The buyer asked to be contacted; speed matters most; consent is clearest      |
| Qualify against a few questions   | Strong    | Short, structured, easy to check afterwards                                   |
| Book, confirm and remind          | Strong    | A calendar tool does the work; little judgement needed                        |
| Re-contact old leads who opted in | Medium    | Works when the list is clean and consent is on file                           |
| Cold calls to strangers           | Weak      | Consent rules bite hardest, and a bad call costs you the account              |
| Negotiate or close                | Weak      | High stakes, long context, and the buyer expects a person who can decide      |
| Train reps by roleplay            | Strong    | No buyer at risk; unlimited practice; every session can be scored on a rubric |

## How do reps train with voice AI?

The rep phones an AI buyer instead of a colleague. The AI plays a persona (a busy operations head, a sceptical CFO), raises objections, and the session is scored against a rubric your managers wrote. Reps get more repetitions than a manager could ever sit through, and managers review the scored sessions instead of running each one.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is voice AI for sales?

Software that holds a spoken conversation to do a sales job. It either talks to buyers (answering leads, qualifying, booking meetings) or plays the buyer so reps can practise.

### How much does an AI sales call cost?

At November 2026 list prices, about 7 to 31 US cents a minute on Retell AI, and 8 cents a minute for ElevenLabs Agents. A three-minute call at Retell's default estimate of 11 cents a minute costs 33 cents.

### Is AI cold calling legal in the US?

Only with consent. The FCC ruled on 8 February 2024 (FCC 24-17) that AI-generated voices are "artificial" voices under the TCPA, so the caller needs prior express consent and must identify itself.

### Does the AI have to say it is an AI?

In the EU, yes from 2 August 2026: the AI Act requires that people are informed when they are interacting with an AI system. Elsewhere, say it anyway: it protects trust, and in the US the caller must identify itself at the start of the call in any case.

### Will voice AI replace sales reps?

The 2026 Salesforce data points the other way: the average seller spends 40% of their time selling, and top performers are 1.7 times more likely to use prospecting agents. Agents take the short, repeatable conversations; people keep the ones that need judgement.
