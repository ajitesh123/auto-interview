**Short answer:** Sales training usually fails for ordinary reasons: it is delivered as a one-off event, reps get little chance to practise before live calls, managers do not reinforce it, and almost nobody measures whether behaviour on real calls changed. The famous failure and forgetting percentages quoted about this mostly cannot be traced to a primary study. What can be checked is that companies keep spending heavily on training, that forgetting is a real and well-replicated effect, and that quota attainment is under pressure. The practical fix is to practise more, spaced over time, and to measure the result on live calls with a control group.

## Why Does Sales Training Fail? The Retention Problem

The best-known explanation is the **forgetting curve**, first described by Hermann Ebbinghaus in the 1880s. The effect is real: a 2015 study in PLOS ONE reported "a successful replication of Ebbinghaus' classic forgetting curve from 1880 based on the method of savings" ([Murre & Dros, 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4492928)).

That study is worth reading for what it does not say. It followed one subject learning lists of nonsense syllables. It does not give forgetting percentages for sales skills, product knowledge or objection handling, and nobody should quote it as if it did.

### Where the "84% forgotten" figure comes from

The widely repeated claim that reps lose 84% of training within 90 days traces back to an [ATD blog post from March 2014](https://www.td.org/content/atd-blog/five-ways-to-improve-the-roi-of-your-sales-training-program), which says: "According to one study, approximately 50% of learning content is not retained after five weeks, and after 90 days, 84% of what was initially learned is lost." The post names no study, and we could not find one behind it. Other popular figures, about how much is forgotten within a day or how little training is applied on the job, have the same problem. We have left them out.

The honest summary: people forget what they do not use, and they forget faster when the material is new and unpractised. Nobody has a reliable single number for how much a sales team forgets.

### Why traditional training methods struggle

**Information overload**: Long, intensive sessions try to compress weeks of material into hours or days.

**Passive learning**: Lectures and slide decks give reps little chance to retrieve and use what they heard.

**No application opportunities**: If a rep does not use a new talk track soon, on a practice call or a live one, it tends to fade before it becomes habit.

## How Much Does Corporate Training Cost?

Spending is large and rising. According to [Training magazine's 2025 Training Industry Report](https://trainingmag.com/2025-training-industry-report), "U.S. training expenditures jumped 4.9 percent to \$102.8 billion in 2025." Per-learner spend went up too: "\$874 per learner this year compared with \$774 per learner in 2024."

At the same time, time spent in training fell: "employees received 40 hours of training per year vs. 47 hours last year." More money and fewer hours means each hour of training has to do more work, which makes it more important to know whether it is working.

These figures cover all corporate training in the US, not sales training alone. We did not find a reliable public figure for sales training spend specifically.

### The opportunity cost

Training also costs selling time. A simple way to estimate it for your own team:

**Opportunity cost = training hours ÷ selling hours per month × monthly gross margin target per rep**

Use your own numbers for hours and margin. The point is to include this cost when you decide whether a programme paid for itself.

## Why Sales Training Programs Fail: The Usual Causes

Pressure on sales teams is real. In [Salesforce's July 2024 sales AI statistics](https://salesforce.com/news/stories/sales-ai-statistics-2024), "67% of sales reps don't expect to meet their quota this year, and an even larger 84% missed it last year." [The Bridge Group's 2025 SDR report](https://bridgegroupinc.com/research/sdr-tenure-and-ramp-time) puts average SDR ramp at 3.0 months and finds 60% of SDRs at quota, the lowest in the study's history.

Those numbers do not prove training is the cause, but they explain why leaders want training to show results. The most common reasons it does not:

### 1. Failure to define business needs

Many programmes start from a vendor's curriculum rather than an analysis of where deals are actually lost. Without knowing which behaviours need to change, training becomes a solution in search of a problem.

### 2. Generic approaches

One-size-fits-all content ignores the rep's market, product and buyer. B2B sales in particular depend on situation-specific talk tracks.

### 3. Lack of reinforcement

When the workshop ends and nobody follows up, new skills have no chance to become habits. Reinforcement through coaching, practice and call review is where most of the lasting change happens.

### 4. Disconnect from real-world application

Training often happens in settings that look nothing like a real call. Back in their territories, reps fall back on what they already know.

## Is Sales Coaching Effective? The Coaching Gap

Coaching is the bridge between a workshop and behaviour on calls, but in many teams it is the weakest link. Front-line managers are often promoted from top reps and given little training in how to coach. They end up coaching on intuition, or reviewing the pipeline instead of the conversation.

We have not found a primary study that cleanly measures how win rates change with weekly coaching time, so we do not quote one.

### Common coaching failures

**Treating symptoms rather than root causes**: Coaching addresses the lost deal without diagnosing the underlying skill, knowledge or motivation gap.

**Lack of customisation**: The same feedback is given to every rep regardless of what they need.

**Insufficient accountability**: Buying an online course and calling it coaching, with nobody checking whether behaviour changed.

## What Are the Best Sales Training Methods?

These approaches follow from what is known about learning and are straightforward to test in your own team:

### 1. Microlearning and spaced repetition

Break content into short modules delivered over weeks, and revisit key skills at increasing intervals rather than once.

### 2. Post-training reinforcement

Follow every workshop with:

- Coaching conversations
- Peer or AI roleplay practice
- Applied exercises tied to real deals

### 3. Performance-driven measurement

Measure behaviour change and business outcomes, not completion rates. The Kirkpatrick Model's Level 3 is a useful definition: "Behavior measures the degree to which the target audience performs the critical behaviors in its environment and is supported in and accountable for its performance" ([Kirkpatrick Partners](https://www.kirkpatrickpartners.com/the-kirkpatrick-model/)).

### 4. Manager development

Front-line managers are the main channel for ongoing skill development. Teaching them to coach on calls, not just on pipeline, multiplies whatever the training budget buys.

### 5. Technology integration

Use practice tools, call recording and analytics selectively, where they make reinforcement and measurement easier.

## Sales Training ROI: Measure Your Investment

### ROI calculation formula

**Training ROI = (Benefits - Training Costs) / Training Costs × 100**

### Inputs

- Programme costs (instructor fees, materials, technology)
- Employee time (hours × loaded hourly cost)
- Opportunity cost (selling time lost)

### Outputs

- Change in win rate or deals closed
- Additional gross margin
- Rep retention
- Time to productivity for new hires

There is no trustworthy public benchmark for "typical" sales training ROI, so we have removed the benchmark ranges earlier versions of this post quoted. Your own before-and-after numbers, with a control group, are worth more than any industry average.

The ROI Institute's guidance is the key rule: "Use at least one method to isolate the effects of a project" ([ROI Institute](https://roiinstitute.net/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/Fold-Out-Model.pdf)). Without isolating the effect, a good quarter will make any programme look successful.

## Industry-Specific Training Challenges and Solutions

### Technology/SaaS

**Challenges:** complex products, long sales cycles, multiple stakeholders, frequent product updates.

**Solutions:** short modules for product updates, roleplay for technical objections, ongoing certification, learning tied to the CRM.

### Healthcare

**Challenges:** regulatory compliance, complex approval processes, clinical and administrative decision makers, high-stakes decisions.

**Solutions:** compliance-focused modules, case-based learning, multi-stakeholder selling practice, regular regulatory updates.

### Financial services

**Challenges:** complex products and rules, high trust requirements, crowded markets with similar offerings.

**Solutions:** risk and suitability training, trust-building conversations, differentiation practice, compliance training.

### Manufacturing/industrial

**Challenges:** technical specifications, long-term relationships, supply chain considerations, price-sensitive buyers.

**Solutions:** technical specification training, account management programmes, value-based selling, supply chain fluency.

### Retail

**Challenges:** high turnover, seasonal peaks, service focus, short point-of-sale interactions.

**Solutions:** fast onboarding, service training, seasonal preparation, upselling and cross-selling practice.

## How to tell whether your training worked

Our [sales training ROI calculator and worked example](/blog/ai-sales-roleplay-vs-traditional-sales-training-cost-roi-2026) goes through this in detail. The short version:

1. **Pick one outcome.** For example, conversion from first meeting to second meeting, or win rate on a specific deal type. One outcome you can measure beats ten you cannot.
2. **Keep a control group.** Train half the team now and the other half next quarter. Compare the two halves over the same period, so market conditions affect both equally.
3. **Score live calls at 30 and 60 days on the same rubric.** Use the same rubric before and after training, for both groups, so you are measuring behaviour change (Kirkpatrick Level 3), not satisfaction.
4. **Count margin, not revenue.** Discounting can raise revenue while destroying profit. Measure the gross margin the trained group added, then subtract the full cost of the programme, including selling time.

## Conclusion

Sales training does not fail because of a magic percentage. It fails when it is a one-off event, when reps do not practise, when managers do not reinforce it, and when nobody checks whether behaviour on calls changed. All four are fixable.

### Action plan

1. **Audit current programmes** - What behaviour is each one meant to change, and is anyone checking?
2. **Space the content out** - Short modules and repeated practice instead of one long workshop
3. **Build reinforcement** - Coaching, roleplay and call review after every programme
4. **Develop managers as coaches** - Coaching on calls, not just on pipeline
5. **Measure real outcomes** - A control group, a rubric on live calls, and margin

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### What is the forgetting curve in sales training?

The forgetting curve describes how memory of new material fades over time when it is not used. Hermann Ebbinghaus documented it in the 1880s, and [Murre & Dros (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4492928) replicated it. That research used nonsense syllables and one subject, so it does not tell you what percentage of a sales course a rep forgets. The practical lesson is to space practice out and use new skills soon after learning them.

### Is it true that 84% of sales training is forgotten within 90 days?

We could not confirm it. The figure traces to a [2014 ATD blog post](https://www.td.org/content/atd-blog/five-ways-to-improve-the-roi-of-your-sales-training-program) that cites "one study" without naming it. Treat it as unverified.

### How much do companies spend on training?

According to [Training magazine's 2025 Training Industry Report](https://trainingmag.com/2025-training-industry-report), US training expenditure was \$102.8 billion in 2025, up 4.9 percent, and spend per learner was \$874, compared with \$774 in 2024. This covers all corporate training, not only sales.

### Why does sales training fail?

The usual causes are:

- No clear definition of which behaviours need to change
- Generic, one-size-fits-all content
- No reinforcement after the workshop
- Little practice in conditions that resemble real calls
- No measurement of behaviour on live calls

### What is microlearning and how does it help sales training?

Microlearning breaks content into short modules delivered over time. Combined with spaced repetition, it gives reps more chances to retrieve and use what they learned, which works against forgetting.

### How effective is sales coaching?

It depends on the quality of the coaching and the manager's own training. We have not found a reliable primary study linking weekly coaching time to win rates, so the best way to judge coaching in your team is to score calls before and after, against a control group.

### What are evidence-based sales training solutions?

- Microlearning and spaced repetition
- Post-training reinforcement through coaching and practice
- Measurement of behaviour on live calls
- Manager development
- Practice and feedback tools, including AI roleplay

### How can organizations measure training ROI?

Pick one outcome, keep a control group, score live calls at 30 and 60 days on the same rubric, and count margin rather than revenue. See our [sales training ROI calculator and worked example](/blog/ai-sales-roleplay-vs-traditional-sales-training-cost-roi-2026).

### What role does AI play in modern sales training?

AI can give reps frequent practice and immediate feedback between workshops, for example by roleplaying a buyer and scoring the call against a rubric. Whether it improves results in your team is something to measure, not assume.

### What's the difference between sales training and coaching?

Training transfers knowledge and introduces skills. Coaching provides ongoing feedback and reinforcement so those skills show up on real calls. Both are needed.

### How to calculate sales training ROI?

Use **Training ROI = (Benefits - Training Costs) / Training Costs × 100**:

1. **Calculate costs**: fees, materials, technology, employee time and lost selling time
2. **Measure benefits**: the change in gross margin for the trained group compared with a control group
3. **Set a baseline**: measure before training starts
4. **Track over time**: check results at several points after training, not just once

### What are the best sales training methods?

1. **Spaced practice** rather than one-off workshops
2. **Roleplay** with peers, managers or an AI buyer
3. **Call review** against a shared rubric
4. **Manager coaching** focused on conversations
5. **Peer learning** from reps who are doing well on the target behaviour

### How to improve sales training effectiveness?

1. Define the behaviour you want to change before training begins
2. Spread content and practice over weeks
3. Reinforce with coaching and roleplay
4. Train managers to coach
5. Tailor content to your market and buyers
6. Measure with a control group and a call-scoring rubric
