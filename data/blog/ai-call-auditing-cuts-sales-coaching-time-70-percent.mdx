## Where Coaching Time Actually Goes Today

### The Manager's Weekly Time Audit

Before you can save time, you need to see where it goes. Here is the breakdown for a typical frontline sales manager with 10 direct reports:

| Activity                                  | Hours/Week | % of 45-hour Week |
| ----------------------------------------- | ---------- | ----------------- |
| Call listening and note-taking            | 8 to 12    | 18 to 27%         |
| Pipeline reviews and CRM                  | 6 to 8     | 13 to 18%         |
| Internal meetings and reporting           | 5 to 8     | 11 to 18%         |
| Hiring and onboarding                     | 3 to 5     | 7 to 11%          |
| Actual coaching conversations             | 5 to 8     | 11 to 18%         |
| Admin and email                           | 4 to 6     | 9 to 13%          |
| Deal support (joining calls, escalations) | 3 to 5     | 7 to 11%          |

Notice the imbalance: **call listening (diagnostic work) consumes as much or more time than the coaching conversations it informs.** This is like a doctor spending 70% of a patient visit running tests and 30% actually treating the patient, every single visit.

### The 70% Diagnostic Burden

When a manager prepares for a coaching session today, the process looks like this:

1. **Select calls to review** (5 to 10 minutes of decision-making, subject to selection bias)
2. **Listen to full calls** (15 to 45 minutes per call, 2 to 3 calls per rep)
3. **Take notes and identify patterns** (5 to 10 minutes per call)
4. **Prioritize coaching topics** (10 to 15 minutes synthesizing across calls)
5. **Prepare coaching talking points** (5 to 10 minutes)

**Total prep time per rep: 45 to 90 minutes.**

The coaching conversation itself: **15 to 30 minutes.**

The ratio is telling. Seventy percent or more of the total coaching effort is diagnostic (listening and analyzing), and only 30% is therapeutic (the actual coaching conversation).

AI call auditing flips this ratio completely.

## The RAPID Coaching Framework for AI-Audited Data

The RAPID framework is designed specifically for managers who have AI call auditing data at their fingertips. Each coaching session follows five steps and takes 20 minutes.

### R: Review AI Insights (2 Minutes)

Before the session, review the rep's AI auditing dashboard. Focus on:

- **Scorecard trends:** Are scores improving, flat, or declining?
- **Biggest gap:** Which scorecard category has the lowest average score?
- **Flagged moments:** What specific call moments did the AI highlight for coaching?
- **Pattern vs. incident:** Is this a recurring weakness or a one-time event?

This replaces 45 to 90 minutes of manual call listening. The AI has already done the diagnostic work.

### A: Align on One Skill (3 Minutes)

Choose ONE skill to focus the entire session on. Not three. Not "general improvement." One specific, observable behavior.

Examples of well-defined coaching targets:

- "Transition from discovery to value presentation using the prospect's own words"
- "Handle the pricing objection using the value-anchor technique before discounting"
- "Ask a second-layer follow-up question after every initial response"

Tell the rep: "Based on your call data this week, here is the one area where a small improvement will have the biggest impact on your conversion rate." Show them the AI data that supports this focus.

### P: Practice with AI Roleplay (8 Minutes)

This is the step that only [Tough Tongue AI](https://app.toughtongueai.com/) enables. During the coaching session, the rep practices the targeted skill against an AI buyer in real-time.

- **Round 1:** Rep practices the skill. Manager observes.
- **Round 2:** Manager provides a specific adjustment. Rep practices again.
- **Round 3:** Rep incorporates the adjustment. Manager evaluates readiness.

Three rounds of focused practice on one skill takes about 8 minutes. The rep leaves the coaching session having already practiced the improvement, not just talked about it.

### I: Implement on Live Calls (Ongoing)

Set a concrete implementation target: "On your next 5 calls today, focus specifically on this one behavior." The target should be small enough to remember without notes and specific enough to be observable.

### D: Debrief on Results (Next Session)

At the next coaching session, open the AI auditing dashboard and review how the rep performed on the targeted skill since the last session. Did scores improve? Did the behavior change appear in transcripts? If yes, celebrate and move to the next skill. If no, dig into why and adjust the approach.

### The RAPID Session Structure

| Step             | Time       | Activity                                        |
| ---------------- | ---------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| **Review**       | 2 min      | Manager reviews AI dashboard insights           |
| **Align**        | 3 min      | Agree on one skill focus with data support      |
| **Practice**     | 8 min      | Three rounds of AI roleplay on the target skill |
| **Implement**    | 2 min      | Set concrete implementation targets             |
| **Debrief plan** | 2 min      | Schedule follow-up, agree on metrics            |
| **Buffer**       | 3 min      | Questions, context, rapport                     |
| **Total**        | **20 min** |                                                 |

Compare this to the traditional coaching flow: 45 to 90 minutes of prep, 30 minutes of conversation (often unfocused), and hope-based follow-up. RAPID delivers more behavior change in less total time because every minute is laser-focused on one thing.

## Common Pitfalls (And How to Avoid Them)

### Pitfall 1: Coaching on Everything at Once

**The mistake:** AI auditing surfaces 5 coaching opportunities per rep per week. The manager tries to address all 5 in one session.

**The fix:** The RAPID framework enforces single-skill focus. Pick the one skill with the highest impact potential and ignore the rest until that one improves. Sequential coaching beats parallel coaching every time.

### Pitfall 2: Using AI Data to Punish, Not Develop

**The mistake:** Managers use AI scores as evidence in disciplinary conversations rather than developmental ones.

**The fix:** Establish a clear policy: AI auditing data is a coaching tool, not a surveillance tool. Compliance violations require intervention, but scorecard scores should drive development conversations, not punishment. Reps who trust the system improve faster.

### Pitfall 3: Skipping the Practice Step

**The mistake:** The coaching conversation stays theoretical. Manager tells the rep what to improve but does not facilitate practice.

**The fix:** The "P" in RAPID is non-negotiable. Every coaching session must include live practice on [Tough Tongue AI](https://app.toughtongueai.com/). Talking about improvement is not improvement. Practicing the improvement is improvement.

### Pitfall 4: Not Tracking Coaching Impact

**The mistake:** Managers coach diligently but never measure whether the coaching changed behavior or outcomes.

**The fix:** Use the coaching scorecard. Review scorecard trend data at every follow-up session. If coached skills are not improving after two cycles, change your coaching approach, not just the coaching volume.

### Pitfall 5: Abandoning the Rhythm

**The mistake:** The RAPID rhythm works for two weeks, then meetings, deals and fires crowd out coaching sessions.

**The fix:** Block coaching time in your calendar as non-negotiable. Treat coaching sessions like customer meetings, not internal meetings. They generate revenue through rep improvement, and they should be protected accordingly.
