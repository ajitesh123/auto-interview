## Why Hinglish roleplay needs its own rules

**Most objection lists online are in English, and none we found pairs Hinglish lines with the rule behind each reply.** We checked the pages that rank for "insurance sales objections Hindi", "sales objection handling in Hindi" and "sales roleplay in Hindi" on 15 Nov 2026: Convin, Juno School, TeleCRM and Awarathon list insurance objections in English only; Convin and Disprz mention IRDAI, but neither gives a Hindi or Hinglish line. A rep who rehearses in English and sells in Hinglish is rehearsing a different call.

Three things change when the call is in Hinglish:

1. **The objection is softer and vaguer.** "Sochke batata hoon" and "WhatsApp pe bhej do" are polite exits, not questions. The rep has to ask what is behind them.
2. **The buyer switches language under pressure.** On hard difficulty the buyer card tells the buyer to reply once in pure Hindi; the rep should follow, not switch to formal English.
3. **Terms need one plain line.** APR, NAV, free look and RERA number mean nothing to many buyers. Line 1 of the rubric rewards explaining each in a sentence.

## How to score a round

**Score every round on the same 4 lines, 1, 3 or 5 points each, and treat a 1 on "Stayed inside the rule" as a failed round whatever the total.** The buyer and one observer score; the rep scores too.

<HinglishRubricTable />

- **16–20:** ready for live calls on that objection.
- **10–15:** close. Rerun now and fix only the lowest line.
- **4–9:** not yet. Rerun tomorrow on easy.

## How to run a session

**Run short rounds of one objection, then swap roles, so every rep also hears the objection from the buyer's side.**

<HinglishSessionFlow />

1. **Write the buyer card in the buyer's words.** Second Nature's help page says that for a non-English simulation all content, persona and evaluation guidelines must be written in that language; the same holds for a human buyer.
2. **Never coach mid-call.** Notes go on the rubric.
3. **Rerun at once.** The second round, fixing one line, is where the change shows.
4. **Keep a never-say list on the wall.** One line per sector, from the table above.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should a telecalling script be in Hindi or Hinglish?

Use the mix your buyers use. Most urban callers code-switch, so scripts written in Hinglish in Roman script ("Sochke batata hoon") read naturally to reps; keep a pure-Hindi version of each key line for buyers who answer in Hindi, and explain every English term in one plain sentence.

### What should I say when a customer says "WhatsApp pe bhej do"?

Agree, then ask one question that tells you what to send, and set a time to talk again. For example: "Zaroor bhejta hoon. Bas itna bata dijiye, aapki day shift hai ya night shift?" Scenario 12 drills it.

### How do you handle "sochke batata hoon"?

Treat it as a polite exit and ask what is left to decide and who else decides: "Kis baat pe sochna hai: price, location, ya family ko ek baar aur dikhana hai?" Never answer with false urgency such as "ye aaj shaam tak bik jayega", and never take a token for a project that is not yet RERA-registered. Scenario 15 drills it.

### Can AI sales roleplay tools work in Hindi?

Several list it: Hyperbound, Second Nature, PitchMonster, Yoodli, Disprz, Outdoo and Tough Tongue AI (our product) each name Hindi on their own page, checked 15 Nov 2026. None of those pages shows a Hinglish sample call, so test one scenario in a free trial before you buy.

### What can't an insurance or mutual fund agent say on a sales call in India?

An insurance agent may not make "a false or misleading statement" about a policy (IRDAI 2024) or, in advertising, compare it unfairly with products that are not comparable, such as an FD. A mutual fund distributor must "abstain from indicating or assuring returns" and explain that funds are not guaranteed (AMFI code). The rules table above links each one.
