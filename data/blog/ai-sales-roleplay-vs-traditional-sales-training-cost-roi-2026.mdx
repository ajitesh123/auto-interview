**Sales training ROI = (first-year gross margin from the sales lift you can pin on training − the programme's fully loaded cost) ÷ fully loaded cost × 100.** In our worked example, a 20-rep team that spends \$113,640 earns \$468,750 of margin back: a 312.5% ROI. The same programme reads 450% if you count revenue instead of margin, and 510.5% if you ignore the 3% lift the untrained reps also got. The difference is the whole game.

<KeyFacts>

- **Formula:** ROI (%) = net programme benefits ÷ programme costs × 100; the benefit-cost ratio (BCR) is benefits ÷ costs ([ROI Institute](https://roiinstitute.net/?p=14250)). A 312.5% ROI is a BCR of 4.12.
- **Isolate the lift:** the ROI Institute's fifth guiding principle is "Use at least one method to isolate the effects of a project." The cleanest is a control group of reps who train later ([fold-out PDF](https://roiinstitute.net/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/Fold-Out-Model.pdf)).
- **Count margin, first year, full cost:** profit, not bookings; 12 months of benefit only; rep selling time and manager time are costs too.
- **Don't take ROI on everything:** the ROI Institute's target is to take 5–10% of programmes to ROI, the expensive, long-lived, wide-reach ones ([targets](https://roiinstitute.net/roi-repository-for-human-capital-tools-and-templates/setting-your-measurement-and-evaluation-goal-2/)).

</KeyFacts>

## How to measure sales training ROI: the formula in five steps

**ROI is net benefit over cost; the work is in getting an honest benefit and a complete cost.** The ROI Institute calls ROI "a standard financial equation developed through finance and economics", with two versions:

- **ROI (%)** = (programme benefits − programme costs) ÷ programme costs × 100
- **BCR** = programme benefits ÷ programme costs

A BCR of 4.12 and an ROI of 312.5% say the same thing: every \$1 spent came back as about \$4.12 of margin, so about \$3.12 of it is profit on the spend. Several ranking pages mix the two, dividing gain by cost and calling it ROI, which overstates the result by 100 points.

The five steps, applied to sales:

1. **Pick the outcome before the training starts:** bookings per rep, win rate, meetings per 100 dials or ramp months. Write down last year's value for the same months.
2. **Split the team.** Train half now and half next quarter, matched by tenure and territory. The late half is your control group.
3. **Measure both groups over the same period** (a full quarter, or one sales cycle).
4. **Credit only the difference** between the trained group's change and the control group's change, then convert it to gross margin.
5. **Load every cost:** fee, rep hours away from selling, manager hours, design, travel and evaluation. Divide.

## Why your ROI number is probably too high

**Most sales training ROI figures you see online count revenue as if it were profit, credit training with the whole lift, or forget the rep hours spent in the room.** Each shortcut is a choice the ROI Institute's guiding principles rule out:

The ROI Institute's sixth guiding principle says what to do with missing data: "If no improvement data are available for a population or from a specific source, assume that little or no improvement has occurred." A rep who skipped the follow-up survey counts as zero, not as average.

## The five levels: what to measure before ROI

**Kirkpatrick's four levels tell you whether the training landed; Phillips adds a fifth, ROI, on top.** You don't need all five for every programme. The ROI Institute's targets: Level 1 for 90–100% of programmes, Level 2 for 60–90%, Level 3 for 30–40%, Level 4 for 10–20% and ROI for 5–10% ([source](https://roiinstitute.net/roi-repository-for-human-capital-tools-and-templates/setting-your-measurement-and-evaluation-goal-2/)).

Level 3 is where most sales programmes break. A rep can pass a quiz (Level 2) and still pitch the old way on Monday. Scoring the same skill on live calls 30 and 60 days later is the cheapest early warning you have before the revenue numbers arrive.

## How to isolate the effect of training

**Use a control group when you can, and a conservative estimate when you can't.** The ROI Institute lists eight ways to isolate a programme's effect, including a "Control group arrangement", "Trend line analysis", "Forecasting methods" and "Estimations" ([methodology](https://roiinstitute.net/?p=14250)). For a sales team, three are practical:

| Method        | How to run it in sales                                                                              | When it fits                                            |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Control group | Train half the team this quarter and half next quarter; compare the change in bookings per rep      | 20+ reps (10+ per group), a sales cycle under a quarter |
| Trend line    | Project each rep's last 12 months forward; credit only what lands above the line                    | Stable market, no price or territory changes            |
| Estimate      | Ask each manager what share of the change came from training, then use the lowest defensible figure | Small teams, long cycles                                |

With fewer than ten reps per group, one big deal can swing the result. Compare medians as well as averages, and say so when you report.

## Do reps really forget 84% of training in 90 days?

**Nobody we could find has published the study behind it.** The figure appears on an [ATD blog post from 2014](https://www.td.org/content/atd-blog/five-ways-to-improve-the-roi-of-your-sales-training-program) as "According to one study, approximately 50% of learning content is not retained after five weeks, and after 90 days, 84% of what was initially learned is lost." The post names no study, and we found no primary source from the research firm it is usually credited to.

The underlying idea is real but narrower. Ebbinghaus measured how fast he forgot lists of nonsense syllables in the 1880s, and [Murre and Dros (2015)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4492928) reported "a successful replication of Ebbinghaus' classic forgetting curve from 1880 based on the method of savings", with a single subject. That says memory for unrelated items fades fast without practice. It does not give a percentage for sales skills.

What is measured: a 2003 meta-analysis of organisational training in the _Journal of Applied Psychology_ found effects of similar size at every level, including "0.62 (k = 26, N = 1,748) for results criteria" ([Arthur et al., 2003](https://www.psychologie.uni-mannheim.de/cip/tut/seminare_wittmann/meta_fribourg/Arthur_etal_2003_MA_Trainings_in_organisations.pdf)). Training can move results. Whether yours did is what the control group is for.

## AI roleplay vs classroom training: what changes in the ROI

**The formula is the same; the cost lines and the evidence you get along the way are different.** We found no independent study comparing the two on revenue, so treat any vendor's ROI claim, ours included, as a hypothesis to test with a control group.

| Cost or evidence line      | Classroom workshop                 | AI roleplay practice                        |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Programme fee              | Trainer day rate, per cohort       | Licence per rep or per minute of practice   |
| Rep time away from selling | One or two full days in a block    | Short sessions spread across weeks          |
| Travel and venue           | Often                              | None                                        |
| Manager time               | Running and observing roleplays    | Writing scenarios and reviewing scores      |
| Level 2 evidence           | A quiz or an observed roleplay     | A scored practice call every session        |
| Level 3 evidence           | Call reviews, if someone does them | Same rubric used on practice and live calls |

The calculator works for both: put the licence in "Programme fee", the practice hours in "Training hours per rep", and compare against the workshop quote with the same isolated lift.

## What to do if you are...

- **A VP of Sales with a programme already running:** pull bookings per rep for the trained reps and for anyone not yet trained, same months, and run them through the calculator before you renew.
- **An enablement lead planning a new programme:** stagger the roll-out so half the team is a control group, set the Level 3 rubric before the first session, and agree the outcome metric with finance.
- **A founder with five reps:** skip ROI; track ramp months for each new hire and score five calls a month per rep on one rubric. That tells you more than a percentage built on five people.
- **An L&D team asked to "prove ROI" on everything:** use the ROI Institute's targets: reaction surveys on almost everything, ROI on the 5–10% of programmes that cost the most or reach the most people.
- **In India:** price the programme and the rep hour in rupees and use gross margin per rep, not CTC; switch the calculator to the India example and replace its numbers with yours.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do you calculate ROI on sales training?

Take the first-year gross margin from the sales lift you can attribute to training, subtract the fully loaded cost of the programme, divide by that cost and multiply by 100. Attribute the lift with a control group of reps trained later, and include rep and manager time in the cost.

### What is a good ROI for sales training?

There is no published benchmark we would trust. Any positive ROI calculated the conservative way (margin, isolated lift, first year, full cost) means the programme paid for itself in a year. Compare it with the return your finance team expects from other spending, and report the break-even lift beside it.

### What is the difference between ROI and BCR?

BCR is benefits divided by costs; ROI is net benefits divided by costs, as a percentage. A BCR of 2.0 equals an ROI of 100%. If a page reports "300% ROI" by dividing revenue by cost, it is reporting a BCR of 3.0, which is an ROI of 200%, and on revenue, not margin.

### How long after training should you measure ROI?

Measure behaviour on live calls at 30 and 60 days, and results over a full quarter or one sales cycle, whichever is longer. Count only the first year of benefit, as the ROI Institute's ninth guiding principle says for short-term programmes.

### Can you measure sales training ROI without a control group?

Yes, with a trend line or an estimate. Ask managers what share of each rep's change came from training, take the lowest figure you can defend, and say in your report that it is an estimate. The ROI Institute's principles say to adjust estimates "for potential errors of estimation" and choose the most conservative option.

### Should I use revenue or profit in the ROI formula?

Profit. Revenue counts the cost of what you sold as if it were a return on the training. In our worked example, using revenue turns a 312.5% ROI into 450%.

### Is the Kirkpatrick model enough to prove ROI?

No. Kirkpatrick's Level 4 shows whether results moved; it does not convert them to money or set them against cost. The Phillips method adds that fifth step: isolate the effect, convert it to money, and compare it with the fully loaded cost.

### Do AI roleplay tools have a better ROI than classroom training?

We have not found an independent study that compares them on revenue. AI roleplay usually takes less selling time per session and needs no travel, and it gives you a score on every practice call; classroom training gives a group experience with a live coach. Run both through the calculator with your own quotes and the same isolated lift.
