**Objection handling is answering a buyer's "no" with a short acknowledgement, one question that finds what the "no" really means, and a request for a small next step, instead of a pitch. Keep it under 20 seconds: in Gong's study of 67,149 recorded demos, the average rep answered an objection with a 21.45-second monologue, while successful reps paused longer and answered with questions. Below: what to say to 19 common objections, the one line that means stop, and a free checker for your own answer.**

<KeyFacts>

- **Agree, ask, then answer.** Gong's successful reps "pause longer after objections" and respond "by asking questions"; average reps sped up and talked for 21.45 seconds ([Gong, 2018](https://www.gong.io/blog/handling-sales-objections)).
- **Most cold-call objections are reflexes.** Dismissive 49.5%, situational (price, budget, bandwidth) 42.6%, existing solution 7.9%; the top 5 objections are 74% of all ([Gong, 31 Jul 2024](https://www.gong.io/blog/we-found-the-top-objections-across-300m-cold-calls-heres-how-to-handle-them-all)).
- **"Don't call me again" is not an objection.** UK rules say you stop, B2B calls included; US rules say the same for consumer calls (business calls are mostly exempt). Stop anyway, and add the number to a do-not-call list.
- **Reps want more practice.** In a Salesforce survey of 4,050 sales professionals, 41% of sales reps said they don't get enough opportunities to roleplay before customer calls, and 46% said they rarely get feedback on their sales conversations ([Salesforce State of Sales, 2025 fieldwork](https://www.salesforce.com/en-us/wp-content/uploads/sites/4/documents/reports/sales/salesforce-state-of-sales-report-2026.pdf)).

## The 5 moves of a good objection answer

**A good answer to an objection takes under 20 seconds: pause, agree, ask one question, answer in one sentence, ask for a small next step.** Every framework below is a version of this. The order matters because an objection on a cold call is usually a reaction to being interrupted, not to your offer.

<ObjectionAnswerFlow />

- **Pause and agree.** Gong's 2024 method starts with "Agree With The Objection", because "it feels really dumb to keep fighting someone who isn't fighting you" ([Gong](https://www.gong.io/blog/we-found-the-top-objections-across-300m-cold-calls-heres-how-to-handle-them-all)). Agreeing means "That's fair", not "You're right, we're not for you".
- **Ask before you answer.** Sandler's advice: "Instead of defending, question their objection with empathy and neutrality" ([Sandler, 7 May 2025](https://go.sandler.com/bestsalespeople/insights/blog/categories/sales-process/objections-aren-t-the-end-they-re-the-beginning-/)). "Too expensive" compared with what? "No budget" this quarter, or ever?
- **Answer short, about them.** One sentence with one concrete detail: a number, a date, a team size. Gong's average reps went on for 21.45 seconds; that is the length to stay under.
- **Check, then ask for the next step.** Richardson's process ends with "Check for feedback" to confirm the answer addressed the concern, then "Repeat the process" ([Richardson](https://www.richardson.com/sales-resources/how-to-overcome-objections-in-sales/)). On a cold call, the next step is small: one more question, two times to choose from, a name.

## When "no" means stop

**If a prospect asks you not to call again, you stop: in the UK that is a rule even for business calls, and in the US it is for consumer calls.** Do not "handle" it. Confirm, apologise, end the call and add the number to your do-not-call list the same day.

<StopRulesTable />

The "Where'd you get my number?" card can turn into a stop request, so answer it straight: name the source and offer to take the number off your list. The ICO's advice is to keep a suppression list rather than delete the record: "Don't just delete all record of them."

## Objection handling frameworks compared

**LAER, Richardson's 5 steps, Sandler, Gong's 3 steps and feel-felt-found all do the same three things: take the objection on board, find the real concern with a question, then answer it.** They differ in where they put the weight. Pick one and train the whole team on it, so that call reviews use the same words.

<ObjectionFrameworkTable />

Carew International's CEO describes LAER as "a key technique for managing objections" ([Selling Power, 18 Dec 2023](https://sellingpower.com/blog/a-simple-four-step-approach-to-mastering-sales)). Feel, felt, found is the most quoted and the easiest to overuse: it only works if the "others who felt the same" are real customers you can name. We found no primary source for who first taught it.

## How to train SDRs on objection handling

**Train objection handling like a sport: one objection at a time, out loud, scored on the same rules, repeated within the week.** Reading a script list does not change what a rep says under pressure. The Bridge Group's 2025 report on 351 B2B companies puts average SDR ramp at 3.0 months ([Bridge Group](https://bridgegroupinc.com/research/sdr-tenure-and-ramp-time)), so the brush-offs need to be automatic in the first few weeks.

1. **Week 1: Not interested · Send me some information · I'm in a meeting.** Each rep writes an answer, scores it in the checker above, then says it out loud 5 times. Ready when: 14/16 or more on the checker and under 20 seconds timed.
2. **Week 2: Call me in 6 months · Not my responsibility · Is this a cold call?** Pairs: one reads the objection cold, the other answers; swap after each. Ready when: answers come without notes, still under 20 seconds.
3. **Week 3: Too expensive · No budget · No bandwidth.** Full roleplay rounds with a buyer card and a rubric (see our [roleplay scenarios](/blog/12-realistic-sales-role-play-scenarios-sdr-mastery-2026)). Ready when: 16/20 on the rubric.
4. **Week 4: We do it in-house · Competitor · Stuck in a contract.** Review 3 recorded live calls and mark every objection and what came next. Ready when: a question follows most objections on real calls.

The plan is ours, not a published standard. Measure it on real calls, not on practice scores: count how often an objection is followed by a question from the rep, and how often the call reaches a next step. Both can be marked by a manager listening to recordings.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is objection handling in sales?

Objection handling is how a seller responds when a buyer pushes back ("too expensive", "not interested", "we use someone else"). A good response acknowledges the objection, asks a question to find the real concern, answers it briefly and asks for a small next step, instead of arguing or pitching harder.

### What are the most common sales objections?

On cold calls, Gong's 300M+ call study groups them into dismissive (49.5%: "Not interested", "Send me some information", "Call me in 6 months"), situational (42.6%: "Too expensive", "No budget", "No bandwidth") and existing solution (7.9%: "We do it in-house", a competitor, a contract). The top 5 objections make up 74% of all of them.

### How do you handle "send me an email"?

Agree, then ask one question that decides what you send: "Happy to. So it isn't a generic brochure, which matters more to you, ramp time or win rates?" Then ask for a time to go through it. Taking the email without a question usually ends the conversation.

### How do you handle "not interested" on a cold call?

Agree that the call was unexpected, give one line on the problem you solve for people like them, and ask whether it applies: "That's fair, you didn't expect my call. I called because new reps often take about three months to ramp. Is ramp time something you're working on this year?" If they say no again, thank them and end the call.

### What is the LAER model?

LAER stands for Listen, Acknowledge, Explore, Respond. It is Carew International's model for handling objections: listen fully, acknowledge the concern, explore it with questions, and only then respond.

### What is feel, felt, found?

A three-step answer: "I understand how you feel. Other clients felt the same. What they found was…" It works only when the other clients are real and you can name what they found; used as a script, buyers recognise it.

### How long should an answer to an objection be?

Under 20 seconds out loud, which is about 50 words. In Gong's study of 67,149 demos, average reps answered objections with a 21.45-second monologue, while successful reps paused and asked questions.

### How do you train SDRs on objection handling?

One objection at a time, out loud, scored on the same rules every time, and repeated later in the week. Start with the brush-offs, move to price and budget in week 3, and judge progress on recorded live calls: does a question follow the objection, and does the call reach a next step?
