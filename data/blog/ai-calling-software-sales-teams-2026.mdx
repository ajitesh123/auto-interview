**AI calling for sales means one of two things: an AI voice agent that phones leads and talks to them itself, or an AI-assisted dialer that dials many numbers so a human rep talks only when someone answers. Published agent prices run from about 4.5 to 32 US cents a minute (11 Nov 2026). What decides your cost per meeting is how many calls are answered and how many conversations book, not the per-minute price. In the US, an AI voice needs the person's prior consent before it calls them; the UK, the EU, India, the UAE and Australia each add their own rules.**

<KeyFacts>

- **Two kinds of tool.** Voice agents (Retell AI, Bland AI, ElevenLabs, Bolna and others) do the talking. AI dialers (Orum, Nooks, CloudTalk, Aircall, JustCall, Dialpad) keep a rep on the call and remove the waiting.
- **Price:** 8 agent platforms publish a per-minute price, from Bolna's 4.5 cents to Air.ai's 32 cents for inbound. Most add telephony on top.
- **The funnel decides the cost.** On Gong's average cold-call rates, 1,000 dials book about 2.5 meetings; at the rates of Gong's top reps, about 22. At the same 11-cent price, that is \$28.12 against \$4.12 per meeting.
- **The law:** the FCC treats AI voices as "artificial" under the TCPA (FCC 24-17, February 2024). The UK's ICO says consent for live calls "is not enough" for automated ones. The EU AI Act's disclosure duty applies from 2 August 2026.

</KeyFacts>

Two things changed recently. In September 2026 TRAI defined automated calls as those "initiated by an application, software system or automated platform without direct human dialing", and they must now be declared to the telecom provider in advance; undeclared ones are treated as unsolicited commercial communication. And in the US the FCC proposed (September 2024) that callers disclose AI-generated voices at the start of each call; we found no final rule yet, so disclose anyway. Our longer guides cover [the US](/blog/ai-calling-usa-tcpa-fcc-stir-shaken-compliance-guide-2026), [India](/blog/ai-calling-india-dpdp-trai-dlt-compliance-complete-guide-2026), [the UAE](/blog/ai-calling-regulations-dubai-uae-cabinet-resolution-56-2026) and [Australia](/blog/ai-calling-regulations-australia-acma-dncr-2026).

## Which sales calls should an AI make, and which should a person?

Our view, from the rules above and the cost maths. Give the agent short, structured calls to people who expect them. Keep people on calls where judgement or a relationship decides the outcome.

| Call                                        | Who should make it      | Why                                                                |
| ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Call back a new inbound lead within minutes | AI agent                | The buyer asked; speed matters; consent is clearest                |
| Qualify against 3 to 5 questions            | AI agent                | Structured, easy to check in the transcript                        |
| Confirm, remind and reschedule meetings     | AI agent                | A calendar task; little judgement needed                           |
| Re-engage old leads who opted in            | AI agent, rep on demand | Works on a clean list; hand over the warm ones live                |
| Cold calls to people who never opted in     | Rep with an AI dialer   | Artificial-voice consent rules; a bad first call loses the account |
| Discovery, negotiation, closing             | Rep                     | High stakes, long context, the buyer wants someone who decides     |

## What should you do at your stage?

Find the line that fits you. Costs are list prices from the tables above.

- **A founder or 1 to 3 reps, mostly inbound.** An agent that calls back every form lead and books meetings. Plan a day to write and test the script. **Cost and next step:** Pay as you go from \$0 a month plus 7 to 14 cents a minute (Retell, Bland Start). Run 50 calls; listen to every one.

- **An SDR team of 5 to 20 doing cold outbound (US/UK).** Keep reps on the phone; add a power or parallel dialer. Plan a week for lists, caller IDs and DNC screening. **Cost and next step:** €15 to €39 per user a month (CloudTalk add-ons), or a quote (Orum, Nooks). Compare answers per rep hour, before and after.

- **A team calling Indian leads.** An India-priced agent, 140-series numbers for promotional calls, A2P use declared. Telecom registration takes longest. **Cost and next step:** About 4.5 to 9 US cents a minute (Bolna, Smallest.ai) plus telephony. Check your numbering with your telecom provider first.

- **A team selling into the EU.** Consent that covers automated calls, and an agent that says it is an AI in its first line. **Cost and next step:** Same per-minute prices plus legal review. Write the disclosure line, then test it on 20 calls.

- **A sales leader whose reps will take AI-booked calls.** Train reps on the hand-over: they inherit a qualified buyer mid-conversation. About an hour per rep a week. **Cost and next step:** Roleplay practice, per seat. Practise the first two minutes of an AI-booked meeting.

## Copy these: disclosure line, consent wording, pilot scorecard

Three things every team needs before the first call. Edit the brackets.

**First line of every call:**

> Hi [first name], this is an AI assistant calling from [company] about [the demo you asked for]. This call may be recorded. Is now a good time for three quick questions?

**Consent tick box on your form** (have your lawyer check it for your country):

> I agree that [company] may call me at the number above, including with automated or AI-generated voice calls, about [product]. Consent is not a condition of purchase. I can opt out at any time by saying stop or replying STOP.

**50-call pilot scorecard.** Score each call yes or no; fix the script after every ten calls.

- [ ] Said it was an AI, who it was calling from, and why, in the first line
- [ ] Stopped talking when the buyer interrupted
- [ ] Asked every qualifying question and logged the answers in the CRM
- [ ] Booked, transferred or ended the call correctly, and honoured any opt-out
- [ ] Said nothing untrue about price, product or terms

**Answer rates start before the call.** Use local, registered numbers, keep calling volumes per number modest, and check how carriers label your numbers; some platforms sell branded caller ID (Retell lists it at 10 cents a call). A number marked as spam drags every rate in the calculator down.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is AI calling for sales?

Using AI on sales phone calls in one of two ways: a voice agent that holds the conversation itself (calling back leads, qualifying, booking meetings), or an AI-assisted dialer that dials many numbers so a rep only talks to people who answer.

### How much does AI calling cost per minute?

At list prices on 11 Nov 2026: Retell AI \$0.07–\$0.31, Bland AI \$0.12–\$0.14, Air.ai \$0.11 outbound, ElevenLabs Agents \$0.08, Aircall \$0.29 pay as you go, and Bolna about 4.5–6 cents. Most add telephony; check what each price covers.

### Is AI cold calling legal in the US?

Only with consent. The FCC ruled in February 2024 (FCC 24-17) that AI-generated voices are "artificial" under the TCPA, so the caller needs prior express consent, and written consent for telemarketing calls to mobiles and residential lines. The call must say who is calling, give a phone number and offer an opt-out.

### Can AI replace SDRs?

Not on cold calls today. Agents fit short calls to people who asked to be contacted; cold calls without consent still need a person in the US and UK. Most teams use agents for callbacks and reminders and keep reps for cold outreach and discovery.

### What is the difference between an AI calling agent and a power dialer?

An agent talks to the buyer. A power or parallel dialer dials for a rep and connects them when someone answers. Agents are priced per minute; dialers per seat per month.

### Do AI sales calls have to say they are AI?

In the EU, yes, from 2 August 2026 under Article 50 of the AI Act. In the US, the caller must identify itself at the start, and the FCC has proposed an AI disclosure rule. Saying it in the first line is the safe default everywhere.
